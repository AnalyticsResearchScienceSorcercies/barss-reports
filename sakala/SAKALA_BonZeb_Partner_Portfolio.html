Part I

Haiti's Charcoal Crisis

94% of Haitians cook with charcoal. The forests are paying the price.

Haiti has lost 98% of its original forest cover. Every year, approximately 51,000 hectares of forest are cleared... much of it burned into charcoal for cooking fuel. This is not a lifestyle choice. It is a survival necessity. 94% of Haitian households depend on charcoal as their primary cooking fuel. The annual charcoal market is valued at approximately $392 million.

The current charcoal supply chain is deforestation-driven, smoke-intensive, and controlled by middlemen who capture most of the margin. Charcoal producers... often the poorest rural families... earn a fraction of the retail price. The environmental damage compounds: deforestation accelerates erosion, reduces rainfall, destroys watersheds, and makes every hurricane more catastrophic.

The math is brutal: Haiti cannot stop using charcoal. There is no grid-scale electricity replacement coming. LPG is imported and unaffordable for most households. The only solution is to change what the charcoal is made from and how it is made. That is exactly what BonZeb does.
