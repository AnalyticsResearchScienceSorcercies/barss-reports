Part II

The Gap No One Has Filled

The global scholarship-to-enterprise pipeline does not exist. This partnership creates it.

The Missing Link in Development

The Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program is the closest global analog to HELP... a merit-based, full-ride scholarship system for high-potential students from underserved communities. It operates at $500M+ scale across 40+ countries. It has transformed access to education for 50,000+ scholars. And it has exactly the same gap HELP does: no graduation-to-enterprise pipeline.

Scholars graduate. They get placed in jobs. The program counts placement rates. But nobody is turning scholars into enterprise owners who create jobs for others in their home communities. Nobody has built the cooperative franchise architecture that converts a scholarship investment into a multiplier. That is what this partnership creates.

HELP's agriculture track makes this alignment structural, not aspirational. HELP's own materials state it plainly: "By educating a new generation of farmers, agronomists, and supply chain leaders, Haiti can grow its own solutions... from the ground up." HELP maintains an especially strong relationship with the Agricultural College at Universite Quisqueya. But today, an agriculture graduate finishes their degree and enters a job market where there are no agro-processing enterprises waiting for them. SAKALA's Harvest Box franchise system is exactly that enterprise.

The Les Cayes expansion reinforces this. HELP opened a new campus at the American University of the Caribbean in 2024-25, with 50 freshmen. HELP is decentralizing from Port-au-Prince into the regions... which is precisely where SAKALA franchise atoms need to be deployed. The geographic strategy is already aligned.

Part III

The Franchise Manager Pipeline

How a HELP fellowship becomes a 10-person enterprise in one step

How It Works

1
HELP Selects
Fellow recruited from countryside. Full tuition, housing, books, stipends, English, IT, leadership.
2
Fellow Trains
Agriculture or business track at UniQ. SAKALA franchise module embedded in career services curriculum.
3
Graduates
Diploma + SAKALA franchise certification. Playbook, data tools, and subscriber pipeline pre-loaded.
4
Returns Home
Launches SAKALA atom in home commune. $3,600 startup funded as "graduation package" by HELP donors.
5
Employs 10+
Manages local youth cooperative. $25K/yr revenue. Community wealth from Day 1.

What the Fellow Brings (Trained by HELP)

  • University degree ... Agriculture, business, or engineering from UniQ or partner institution
  • Leadership training ... HELP's intensive professional development curriculum across 9 departments
  • English capability ... Critical for diaspora subscriber communication and international market access
  • Financial literacy ... Budgeting, planning, and reporting capacity from day one
  • 350+ alumni peers ... Cross-franchise support network already built by HELP over 30 years
  • Hometown trust ... They are from the community. They went to school, came back, and stayed. 80% retention rate.
  • Cooperative instinct ... KOREM alumni already practice 10% income-share for 8 years after graduation. They already think cooperatively.

What SAKALA Provides (Ready at Graduation)

  • 147-document operational playbook ... Launch, staffing, processing, QA, scaling. Everything a franchise manager needs.
  • KoboToolbox data systems ... Mobile-first field data collection with real-time dashboards. Impact tracking per fellow, per commune.
  • Harvest Box subscriber pipeline ... Pre-built diaspora market (975,000 Haitian diaspora mapped across 1,420 ZIP codes) delivering quarterly revenue.
  • Processing standards ... Quality protocols for every product category across 9 franchise types.
  • Market access ... Direct-to-diaspora subscription channel. No middlemen. FL home delivery + MA cooperative pickup at launch.
  • Cooperative ownership structure ... REI model. Subchapter T patronage dividends. Fellow earns management stipend + equity stake.

Why KOREM Makes This a Natural Fit

KOREM is Haiti's first alumni contribution program... a 10% income-share commitment for 8 years after graduation. It has generated over $400,000 and funds 6-7 scholarships annually. This is not charity. It is a cooperative reinvestment mechanism that HELP alumni have already internalized. SAKALA's cooperative profit-sharing model is structurally identical to what KOREM teaches. For a HELP graduate, joining a SAKALA franchise is not a new commitment... it is a continuation of the principle they have already lived for the duration of their education.

The Barbancourt Precedent

This is not HELP's first co-branded partnership. Rhum Barbancourt signed a 5-year deal (2021-2026) providing 3 full scholarships plus a named admissions office. That partnership established the structural template for what we are proposing: a named, co-branded program where a partner organization funds specific components of the HELP pipeline. The SAKALA partnership follows the same architecture... but instead of funding admissions, it funds graduation.
