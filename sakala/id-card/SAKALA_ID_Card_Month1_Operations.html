Dual Interface Operation

Contact mode (chip-and-PIN): Insert card into terminal, enter PIN. Used for high-value transactions, ATM withdrawals, and identity verification at government offices.

Contactless mode (NFC tap): Tap card on terminal. Used for low-value purchases (under threshold set by BOJ, typically $5,000 JMD), transit, and franchise site check-in.

Biometric match: For identity verification scenarios (not payment), the card performs on-card fingerprint matching. The reader captures a fingerprint, sends it to the card's secure element, and the card returns match/no-match. The fingerprint never leaves the card.

03  Enrollment SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)
10 Steps · 15–25 min per enrollee
BEFORE YOU BEGIN EACH DAY: (1) Power on all equipment and verify camera, fingerprint scanner, and software are working. (2) Confirm database connectivity or offline mode is active. (3) Check that consent forms are stocked (minimum 30 per day). (4) Verify photo backdrop is clean and properly positioned.
  1. Youth Arrives at Franchise Enrollment Station
    Greet by name if known. Confirm they are on the enrollment roster for today. If walk-in, add to roster and assign queue number. Estimated wait time communicated. Hand them a consent form to read while waiting.
    Operator
  2. Verify Identity
    Option A (preferred): Present existing CIN (Carte d'Identification Nationale) or other government-issued ID. Operator records CIN number in system. Visually confirms photo match.
    Option B (community witness): If no CIN available, two community witnesses must be present. Each witness must: (a) hold a valid CIN themselves, (b) state under oath they have known the enrollee for at least 1 year, (c) sign the witness attestation section of the consent form. Operator records both witness names and CIN numbers.
    DO NOT PROCEED if neither Option A nor Option B can be satisfied. Reschedule with instructions to bring documents or witnesses.
    Operator
  3. Capture Photo
    Position enrollee on stool or standing mark, 3 feet from camera. White/light background behind them.
    Photo requirements:
    • Front-facing, eyes open, neutral expression
    • No headwear (religious exceptions permitted with face fully visible)
    • No glasses (unless medically required, then no glare)
    • Face fills 70–80% of frame height
    • Even lighting, no harsh shadows
    • Minimum resolution: 600x800 pixels
    Take 2–3 photos. Select best. Show enrollee for approval. Retake if quality check fails (software will flag issues).
    Operator
  4. Capture Fingerprints
    Both index fingers (left and right). Process:
    • Clean scanner surface with alcohol wipe between enrollees
    • Have enrollee wash/wipe hands if dirty or wet
    • Place right index finger flat on scanner. Hold steady for 3 seconds. Software confirms capture quality (NFIQ score >= 3).
    • Repeat with left index finger.
    • If quality check fails: clean finger, reposition, retry up to 3 times.
    • If BOTH fingers fail after 3 attempts each: capture right middle finger as fallback. Document exception.
    NEVER force a poor-quality capture through. Bad biometrics = card that cannot be verified = useless.
    Operator

    DO NOT SKIP THIS STEP. A misspelled name means a card that does not match other ID documents.

  7. Consent Form Signature
    Enrollee signs (or thumbprints if illiterate) the consent form. The consent form covers:
    • Data privacy: What data is collected, how it is stored, who can access it
    • Card terms: Card is cooperative property, must be returned if membership ends
    • Wallet terms: Transaction fees, liability limits, dispute process
    • Biometric consent: Explicit consent for fingerprint capture and storage
    Consent form is bilingual: Kreyol (primary) + French (secondary). Available in both languages at every station.
    Operator signs as witness. If enrollee is under 18, parent/guardian must co-sign.
    Operator
  8. Data Upload / Offline Storage
    If internet available: Enrollment record (photo, fingerprint hashes, personal data) encrypted with AES-256 and uploaded to central SAKALA enrollment database via TLS 1.3. Operator sees green confirmation checkmark.
    If offline: Record encrypted and stored locally on device. Operator sees orange "queued for sync" indicator. At end of day (or when connectivity restored), connect to internet and sync. Software will automatically upload all queued records. Verify all records synced (counter shows 0 pending).
    NEVER leave unsynced records for more than 24 hours. If connectivity is consistently unavailable, physically transport device to a location with internet and sync there.
    Tech Lead
  9. Card Production
    Central printing (default): Once enrollment data reaches central database, it enters the print queue. Print facility (Site 0 or partner facility) produces card within 48–72 hours. Card personalized with laser engraving (name, photo, card number). NFC chip programmed with payment applet, biometric template, wallet address, and membership data. Card placed in sealed envelope labeled with enrollee name and franchise site.
    On-site printing (if printer available): Card produced immediately after enrollment. Enrollee can receive card same day. Quality check by operator before handoff.
    Tech Lead Logistics
  10. Card Delivery and Wallet Activation
    Central printing path: Cards batched by franchise site and couriered to each site within 5 business days of enrollment. Youth notified via SMS when card is ready for pickup. Youth comes to franchise site, presents themselves, and receives card. Proceed to Section 5: Wallet Activation.
    On-site printing path: Card handed to youth immediately after production and quality check. Wallet activation happens on the spot. Youth leaves with fully functional card.
    Operator
ENROLLMENT RECEIPT: Every enrollee receives a receipt (printed or SMS) containing: (1) their enrollment reference number, (2) expected card delivery date, (3) franchise site for pickup, (4) SAKALA support phone number. This receipt is their proof of enrollment until the card arrives.

04  Data Security Protocol
Encryption · Access · Consent · Breach Response
CARDINAL RULE: Raw fingerprint images NEVER leave the enrollment device. Only encrypted hashes are transmitted and stored. If anyone asks you to export, email, or copy fingerprint images... the answer is NO. No exceptions. No "just this once." NO.

Encryption Standards

LayerStandardImplementation
At capture (biometric)AES-256-GCMFingerprint hashed using SHA-256 with salt immediately upon capture. Raw image encrypted in memory, deleted after hash extraction. Hash encrypted with AES-256 for storage.

Consent Framework

What Every Enrollee Signs

The consent form (bilingual: Kreyol + French) covers the following, in plain language:

  • What we collect: Your name, date of birth, address, phone, photo, and fingerprints.
  • Why we collect it: To create your SAKALA cooperative ID and payment card.
  • Who sees it: SAKALA administrators (for card production and membership), Jamaica's NIRA (for identity verification), Bank of Jamaica (wallet address only for payments). Nobody else.
  • What we do NOT do: We never sell your data. We never share your fingerprint images with anyone. We never give your personal information to police, immigration, or any government agency without a court order.
  • How long we keep it: For the life of your card (10 years). You can request deletion at any time by surrendering your card.
  • Your rights: You can see what data we have about you. You can correct errors. You can delete your data (with card surrender). You can withdraw consent at any time.

    Card is in sealed envelope with enrollee's name. Operator verifies identity (visual match to photo on card). Enrollee signs card receipt log. Operator removes card from envelope and hands to enrollee. Enrollee inspects card: name correct? Photo looks right? Any visible damage?
    If any issue: Do not activate. Document the problem. Issue a replacement request.
  2. Download Wallet App or Dial USSD Code
    Smartphone users: Download "SAKALA Wallet" app from Google Play Store (Android) or App Store (iOS). If JAM-DEX integration is live, the BOJ's official JAM-DEX wallet app works too. App size: under 20MB. Works on Android 8+ and iOS 14+.

    No phone at all: Activation can be completed at the enrollment station by the operator on the enrollee's behalf. Enrollee will receive SMS notifications on any phone number provided (can be a family member's phone).
  3. Link Card to Wallet
    Open app (or USSD menu). Select "Activate New Card." Enter:
    • Card number: 16-digit number on front of card
    • Date of birth: DD/MM/YYYY (must match enrollment record)
    System verifies the card number exists and DOB matches. If match: proceed. If no match: retry (check for typos). After 3 failed attempts, card is locked for 1 hour (anti-fraud measure).
  4. Set 4-Digit PIN
    Enrollee chooses a 4-digit PIN. This PIN is used for:
    • Card transactions (chip-and-PIN payments)
    • Wallet app login
    • Cash-out at MonCash agents
    PIN rules: Cannot be 0000, 1111, 1234, or enrollee's birth year. System rejects these automatically.
    Operator guidance: Tell the enrollee to choose a number they will remember but that others cannot guess. NEVER look at or ask for their PIN. If they forget it, there is a reset process (Section 7, troubleshooting).
  5. Wallet Activated — Ready to Use
    Wallet is now active. Enrollee can:
    ActionHowWhere
    Receive payrollAutomatic deposit from SAKALA trustWallet balance
    Pay at merchantsTap card (NFC) or insert + PINAny franchise merchant location with POS terminal
    Cash-outPresent card + PIN at MonCash agentAny MonCash agent location

    View transactionsApp transaction history or USSD *555*2#Anytime
  6. Test Payment: 100 HTG from SAKALA Trust
    Immediately after activation, the SAKALA trust sends a test payment of 100 HTG to the enrollee's wallet. This serves three purposes:
    • Verification: Confirms the wallet is working end-to-end
    • Training: Enrollee sees their first notification, checks their balance, learns the interface
    • Welcome: A small but real gesture that the card has tangible value from minute one
    Operator confirms with enrollee: "Check your balance. Do you see 100 HTG?" If yes: activation complete. If no: troubleshoot (check phone number, check app version, check network connectivity). Escalate to Tech Lead if unresolved in 10 minutes.

08  Training Curriculum for Enrollment Staff
5 Modules · 8 Hours Total · Assessment
TRAINING SCHEDULE: All 5 modules delivered in 1 day (Day 2 of operations). Modules 1–3 in the morning (classroom + demo). Modules 4–5 in the afternoon (hands-on). Assessment at end of day. Nobody deploys to a live enrollment station without passing assessment.

Trainee Prerequisites

  • Literate in Kreyol (reading and writing)
  • Basic computer literacy (can type, use a mouse/touchpad, navigate menus)
  • Trusted by the community (will be handling personal data and biometrics)
  • Reliable (will show up every day, on time, for the full enrollment period)
Module 1 — Card System Overview: What It Is, Why It Matters, Who It Serves 2 Hours

Learning Objectives

  • Explain what the SAKALA ID + Payment Card is and how it differs from a regular bank card
  • Articulate why cooperative identity matters (financial inclusion, dignity, ownership)
  • Name the 3 systems that interact: SAKALA (membership), NIRA (identity), BOJ (payments)

Content

  • What is this card? It is both an identity document AND a payment card. One card, two functions. Like a national ID that can also buy groceries.
  • Why does it matter? Many youth have no formal ID. No ID means: no bank account, no formal employment, no vote, no proof of who you are. This card gives them a verified identity AND a wallet to receive money digitally.
  • Who is it for? First: SAKALA cooperative youth members (25 at Site 0, then expanding). Then: their families. Then: community members at franchise sites.
  • How does it work? [Simplified diagram]: Youth enrolls (photo + fingerprint + data) → Card produced → Card delivered → Wallet activated → Youth receives payroll and makes payments.
  • Your role: You are the front door. Every card starts with you enrolling someone correctly. Bad enrollment = bad card = broken trust. Good enrollment = good card = a youth who just got access to the financial system for the first time.

Activity (30 min)

Each trainee answers: "If your cousin asked you what your new job is, how would you explain it in 30 seconds?" Practice out loud. Group gives feedback. Repeat until everyone can explain it clearly.

Module 2 — Equipment Operation: Camera, Scanner, Software, Troubleshooting 2 Hours

Learning Objectives

  • Set up an enrollment station from a packed case to operational in under 15 minutes
  • Operate the camera, fingerprint scanner, and enrollment software
  • Troubleshoot the 5 most common equipment problems

Content

  • Station setup: Unpack case. Connect laptop to power (UPS first, then wall/solar). Connect USB camera. Connect USB fingerprint scanner. Connect hotspot (power on, wait for signal). Open enrollment software. Log in. Verify all devices detected (green indicators in software).
  • Camera operation: Position subject 3 feet from camera. Frame: face fills 70–80% of frame height. Focus: autofocus, wait for sharp indicator. Lighting: face evenly lit, no harsh shadows. Take 2–3 shots. Select best. Software quality check: green = good, yellow = marginal (retake), red = reject (fix issue and retake).
  • Fingerprint scanner: Clean surface with alcohol wipe. Subject places index finger flat (pad centered on sensor). Hold 3 seconds. Software shows capture quality (NFIQ 1–5, need 3+). If poor: clean finger, reposition, retry. Wet fingers: dry with tissue. Dry fingers: light moisture (breathe on fingertip).
  • Software walkthrough: Live demo of full enrollment flow in software. Each screen, each field, each button. Where to click, what to type, what to select from dropdowns.

Module 3 — Enrollment Process: Step-by-Step with Practice 2 Hours

Learning Objectives

  • Complete a full enrollment (Steps 1–10 from Section 3) in under 20 minutes
  • Handle identity verification (both CIN and witness paths)
  • Achieve photo and fingerprint quality standards on first attempt

Content

  • Live demo: Trainer enrolls a volunteer from start to finish. Trainees observe. Trainer narrates every decision ("I'm checking the photo quality indicator... it's yellow, so I'll retake with better lighting...").
  • Step-by-step walkthrough: Go through Section 3 line by line. Highlight: identity verification (when to use Option A vs B), photo requirements (common mistakes: shadows, too far, too close), fingerprint technique (flat placement, 3-second hold), data entry (spelling, date format, required vs optional fields), consent form (what to explain in Kreyol, where enrollee signs).
  • Common mistakes to avoid:
    • Rushing through identity verification (this is the legal foundation... do it properly)
    • Accepting a marginal photo ("good enough" is not good enough... this photo will be on their card for 10 years)
    • Not reading back all fields to enrollee (they catch errors you don't)
    • Skipping the consent form explanation ("just sign here" is not informed consent)

Activity (45 min)

Pair up. Each trainee enrolls their partner, then switches. Trainer observes and scores: identity check (pass/fail), photo quality (1–5), fingerprint quality (NFIQ score), data completeness (all required fields), consent process (explanation + signature), total time. Debrief: each pair shares what went well and what was hard. Group discusses.

Module 4 — Data Security: What to Protect, What NOT to Do 1 Hour

Learning Objectives

  • Explain what data is sensitive and why
  • List 5 things you must NEVER do with enrollee data
  • Describe the consent form to an enrollee in Kreyol

Content

  • What is sensitive? Fingerprints, photos, names + DOB + address (together), card numbers. Think of it this way: if someone stole this data, could they pretend to be the enrollee? If yes, it is sensitive.
  • The 5 NEVERS:
    1. NEVER copy enrollee data to a USB drive, personal phone, or email
    2. NEVER let anyone photograph the enrollment screen (especially during fingerprint capture)
    3. NEVER share your login credentials with anyone (not even another operator)
    4. NEVER leave the enrollment laptop unlocked and unattended
    5. NEVER discuss one enrollee's data with another enrollee (or anyone not on the team)
  • Consent form practice: Trainer reads through the consent form in Kreyol. Then each trainee practices explaining it to a "simulated enrollee" (another trainee who asks questions like "Who sees my fingerprints?" and "What if I want to delete my data?").

Activity (20 min)

Scenario quiz. Trainer presents 5 situations. Trainee says whether it is OK or NOT OK, and why:
(1) "A youth's mother calls and asks for her son's enrollment data." [NOT OK... verify identity in person first]
(2) "The franchise leader asks to see the enrollment database." [NOT OK... they only get name + membership status]
(3) "Your phone battery is dead so you take a photo with a coworker's phone." [NOT OK... only the enrollment camera, never personal devices]
(4) "A youth wants to see what data you have about them." [OK... it's their right, show them on screen]
(5) "A police officer asks for an enrollee's fingerprint." [NOT OK without a court order... escalate to Admin immediately]

Module 5 — Wallet Activation: Guiding Youth Through Setup 1 Hour

Learning Objectives

  • Walk a youth through wallet activation (Section 5) in under 10 minutes
  • Troubleshoot the 3 most common activation problems
  • Explain wallet features in simple Kreyol

Content

  • Activation walkthrough: Trainer demonstrates full activation on a test card with a test phone. Narrates each step. Points out where youth commonly get confused (entering card number correctly, choosing a PIN they will remember, understanding what the test payment means).
  • Smartphone path vs USSD path: Demonstrate both. Operators must be comfortable with BOTH because you do not know which phone type the youth will have.
  • Feature explanation script (Kreyol):
    • "Kat sa a se kle ou... li ouvri pou ou yon kont ki pou ou." (This card is your key... it opens an account that belongs to you.)
    • "Chak mwa, lajan w ap rive direkteman nan kont ou." (Every month, your money will arrive directly in your account.)
    • "Ou ka achte, voye lajan bay fanmi ou, oswa kenbe li." (You can buy things, send money to your family, or save it.)
  • Common problems: Card number typo (most common), phone too old for app (switch to USSD), no phone at all (operator assists at station), forgot PIN immediately (reset process).

Activity (20 min)

Each trainee activates a test wallet on their own phone (app or USSD). Then: each trainee guides another trainee through activation as if that trainee were a youth who has never used a smartphone. Trainer observes: patience, clarity of instructions, ability to troubleshoot. Trainee must complete 3 activations to pass.
