01 The Caribbean Digital Gap

CARICOM has achieved remarkable political integration... the Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas, the CSME, the Caribbean Court of Justice, CARICOM IMPACS, and most recently, the Caribbean Payment System Switch (CAPSS). Yet three foundational gaps remain:

Gap Impact Annual Cost to Region
No unified digital identity Each country maintains separate, incompatible ID systems. Free movement under CSME requires physical passport presentation. No digital verification layer exists for cross-border transactions. Uncountable (administrative drag across every sector)
No unified payment system Cross-border payments route through US correspondent banks. 3-7% transaction fees. 2-5 day settlement. CAPSS exists but has no consumer-facing product. $150-350M in unnecessary intermediation fees
$5 billion food import dependency CARICOM imports 60-80% of food consumed. Intra-regional agricultural trade is negligible. Payment friction is a primary barrier. $5B annual capital outflow + food security vulnerability

The Compounding Cost

A farmer in Jamaica grows produce. A hotel in Barbados needs it. The payment to transfer $500 for that produce costs $15-35 in fees, takes 3-5 days to settle, requires correspondent bank routing through New York, and neither party can digitally verify the other's identity or business registration. The friction is not technical. It is infrastructural. CAPSS was built to solve it. What is missing is the consumer-facing product layer.

Approximately 40% of the Caribbean population remains financially excluded... no bank account, no digital wallet, no access to the formal payment system. In Haiti, the figure exceeds 80%. These are not people who reject financial services. They are people whom financial services have not reached.

03 How It Works

The enrollment-to-activation pipeline:

Step Action Technology Time
1. Enrollment Citizen visits SAKALA franchise site or national registration office. Biometric capture (fingerprint + photo). Basic KYC data collected. Mobile enrollment kit ($200-400) 15 minutes
2. Card Issuance NIDS-compatible NFC smart card printed or shipped. Biometric template stored on-card (Match-on-Card). Unique ID number assigned. Dual-interface smart card ($3-8) Same day or 3-5 days (mail)
3. CBDC Wallet JAM-DEX wallet created automatically at enrollment. Linked to card's NFC secure element. Tiered KYC determines wallet limits. BOJ Wallet Provisioning API Instant (at enrollment)
4. International Payment Visa/Mastercard prepaid applet activated on card. Enables diaspora remittances, international e-commerce, ATM withdrawal. EMV contactless, card network rails Instant (at enrollment)
5. CAPSS Cross-Border Card registered for CARICOM cross-border transactions. Instant settlement between any two CAPSS-connected nations. CAPSS gateway integration Instant (at enrollment)

Result: In 15 minutes, a citizen who had no ID and no bank account now holds a card that proves their identity, stores digital currency, accepts international payments, and transacts instantly across 16 CARICOM nations. The card costs less than a meal.

04 Phase 1: Haiti Pilot

Why Haiti First

Haiti is the CARICOM member with the highest financial exclusion (80%+), no national ID system, and no CBDC. If the card works in Haiti... with its infrastructure constraints, security challenges, and institutional gaps... it works everywhere in the Caribbean. Haiti proves the floor. The rest of CARICOM proves the ceiling.

CBDC Interoperability

The Bahamas (Sand Dollar), the OECS (DCash), and Jamaica (JAM-DEX) all have operational CBDCs. Trinidad & Tobago and Barbados are exploring their own. The CARICOM Card creates a single physical interface for all Caribbean CBDCs. One card holds whichever CBDC is native to the holder's country, and CAPSS handles the cross-border conversion. The citizen never sees the complexity.

08 CUCAER Alignment

The Caribbean Universities Consortium for Agricultural Education and Research (CUCAER) connects UWI, UTT, and agricultural training institutions across CARICOM. SAKALA's franchise operators receive cooperative management certification through this consortium. The ID+payment card creates a direct link between agricultural education and economic participation.

CUCAER Function Card Integration Outcome
Student enrollment CARICOM Card serves as student ID + stipend wallet Unified credentialing across CARICOM universities
Certification SAKALA cooperative management cert stored on card Portable credentials recognized regionally
Research data Card transactions generate agricultural trade data Real-time crop yield, price, and trade flow data for research
Extension services Card enrollment = registered farmer database Direct communication channel for advisories, subsidies, inputs
Grant disbursement Research grants and farmer subsidies paid to card wallet Eliminates cash handling, enables audit trail

The card does not just enable trade. It enables the data infrastructure that CUCAER needs to transform Caribbean agriculture from subsistence to surplus. Every transaction is a data point. Every data point informs research. Every research output improves the next growing season.

What This Is Not

This is not a request for CARICOM to build new technology. This is a request for CARICOM to connect the technology its member states have already built. Jamaica built the ID. Jamaica built the CBDC. CARICOM built CAPSS. SAKALA built the ground network. Nigeria proved the concept. What is missing is a political decision to integrate them.

The Caribbean has spent decades building the components of digital public infrastructure. A single political decision... one line item on the Heads of Government agenda... connects those components into the most advanced unified digital identity and payment system in the developing world. Sixteen countries. Twenty million people. One card.
