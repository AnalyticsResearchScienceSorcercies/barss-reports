3. The Opportunity

A mandate, a meal program, and a missing piece

Summits Education operates 39-42 schools in Haiti's Central Plateau, serving over 10,000 students. Through the partnership with Mary's Meals, 98% of those students receive a daily meal. That meal program is one of the most effective in Haiti.

But the ground is shifting. The Haitian government has mandated local food sourcing for school meal programs:

Deadline Requirement Status
2026 50% of school meal ingredients sourced locally This year
2030 100% of school meal ingredients sourced locally 4 years away

This mandate is an opportunity, not a burden. It means the meal program that already works can now also create Haitian jobs, support Haitian farmers, and circulate money within Haitian communities. But it requires a local supply chain that can deliver quality produce at scale, reliably, to the Central Plateau.

That supply chain is SAKALA. 160 youth already farming across 7 departments. Moringa at 8 sites. Cassava, fruit trees, peanuts, and more. Not a plan to grow food. Food growing right now.

6. The Pilot

5-10 schools. One season. Prove it works.

We are not asking Summits to overhaul its meal program. We are asking for a pilot: SAKALA supplies locally grown produce to 5-10 Summits schools for one growing season. We track quality, cost, reliability, and student health outcomes together. If it works, we scale. If something needs adjustment, we adjust.

Month 1-2
Setup
Identify 5-10 pilot schools in Central Plateau. Assess kitchen infrastructure and current meal composition. Activate or expand nearest SAKALA production site. Align planting schedule with school calendar.
Month 3-4
First Harvest & Delivery
First moringa and cassava deliveries to pilot schools. Moringa powder integrated into existing porridge or rice recipes. Track delivery reliability, product quality, and kitchen feedback. Coordinate with Mary's Meals on logistics.
Month 5-6
Full Rotation
Expand product range: add peanut butter, pigeon peas, seasonal vegetables. Full local sourcing rotation across pilot schools. Student health and attendance data collection begins.
Month 7-8
Assessment
Joint review: Summits + SAKALA. Cost comparison vs. imported supply. Quality metrics. Reliability data. Student health indicators. Decision point: scale to full network or refine approach.

9. Summits Academy

A college prep school with a living curriculum

Summits is building something new: Summits Academy, a college prep school designed to prepare Haiti's best students for university and beyond. SAKALA sees a secondary opportunity here that goes beyond meal supply.

What if Summits Academy students didn't just learn about cooperatives in a textbook? What if they participated in one?

The idea: SAKALA as a living entrepreneurship curriculum for Summits Academy. Students learn cooperative economics, agricultural business, supply chain management, and financial literacy by participating in a real cooperative that supplies food to their own school network. Not theory. Practice.

This is a Year 2 conversation, not a Day 1 ask. But the alignment is worth noting: Summits Academy wants to prepare students for the real economy. SAKALA is a piece of the real economy that those students can touch, measure, and help build.
