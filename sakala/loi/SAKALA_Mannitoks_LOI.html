SAKALA × BARSS Relaunch — Mannitoks

Letter of Intent

Payment Processing Infrastructure & Cooperative Mobile Platform Partnership
Non-Binding Letter of Intent
This Letter of Intent ("LOI") is entered into as of the date last signed below, by and between SAKALA × BARSS Relaunch, a cooperative social enterprise represented by Daniel Tillias and Wesley Bertil (collectively, "SAKALA"), and Mannitoks, a fintech platform represented by its Founder & CEO, Chris Chérubin ("Mannitoks"). SAKALA and Mannitoks are referred to individually as a "Party" and collectively as the "Parties."
1. Purpose

Mannitoks shall provide payment processing infrastructure, digital wallet services, and API access to SAKALA. SAKALA shall provide a merchant network of cooperative franchise locations, a subscriber pipeline of up to 975,000 mapped diaspora members, and native mobile application development. The Parties intend to build an integrated cooperative financial platform serving Haitian diaspora communities and Haiti-based franchise operators.

2. Term

This LOI shall remain in effect for a period of three (3) years from the date of execution, automatically renewable for successive one-year periods unless either Party provides written notice of non-renewal at least ninety (90) days prior to the end of the then-current term.

3. Mannitoks Obligations

Integrate SAKALA cooperative membership IDs with the Mannitoks wallet system, enabling each cooperative member to hold a unique digital wallet linked to their membership credentials.

Process payroll disbursements from the SAKALA trust to individual youth wallets, ensuring timely and transparent distribution of cooperative earnings.

Provide API access sufficient for BARSS to build a native mobile application (iOS and Android) that interfaces with Mannitoks wallet and payment infrastructure.

Enable a Community Wealth Fund Surcharge of $1.00 per transaction, configured as default-on with member opt-out capability.

Provide merchant onboarding services for up to 344 SAKALA franchise locations, including POS integration and merchant support.

Disclose the identity of all banking partner(s) to SAKALA for the purpose of BAM BAM due diligence review, as further described in Section 8.

Critical Requirement — Section 3(f)

SAKALA cannot partner with any entity that routes funds through oligarch-controlled Haitian banks. Full banking partner disclosure is a condition precedent to the execution of any binding agreement.

4. SAKALA Obligations

Route all franchise payroll disbursements through the Mannitoks platform.

Build a native mobile application (iOS and Android) on the Mannitoks API, developed by the BARSS tech team at no cost to Mannitoks.

Promote Mannitoks as the preferred payment platform to SAKALA's 975,000 mapped diaspora subscribers.

Provide 344 franchise sites as merchant acceptance points for Mannitoks wallet transactions.

Manage the allocation and disbursement of the Community Wealth Fund in accordance with SAKALA's cooperative governance procedures.

Support Mannitoks' US regulatory pathway, including FinCEN Money Services Business (MSB) registration and applicable state licensing requirements, through data sharing and cooperative infrastructure access as reasonably requested.

5. Financial Terms
Community Wealth Fund Surcharge: $1.00 per transaction, directed in full to the SAKALA Community Wealth Fund.

Mannitoks shall retain its standard platform fee of $3.50–$4.00 per transaction, which is separate from and in addition to the Community Wealth Fund Surcharge. No additional fees, revenue shares, or platform costs shall be charged to SAKALA beyond the standard platform fee.

BARSS shall build the native mobile application at no cost to Mannitoks, recognizing this development as SAKALA's investment in the partnership infrastructure.

6. Smart Card Integration

SAKALA shall procure an initial order of 5,000 NFC-enabled smart cards for distribution to cooperative members and franchise operators. Each card shall be linked to the holder's Mannitoks wallet via NFC tap or QR code scan. Card specifications, including chip type, branding, security features, and production vendor, shall be determined jointly by the Parties.

7. Data Ownership

Transaction Data: All transaction data generated through the partnership shall be jointly owned by both Parties, with each Party retaining the right to use such data for its own operational and analytical purposes.

User Identity Data: All cooperative member identity data, including personal information, membership records, and cooperative governance data, shall be owned exclusively by SAKALA.

Anonymized Analytics: Mannitoks may use anonymized and aggregated transaction analytics for platform improvement, product development, and investor reporting, provided that no individual member or franchise location is identifiable.

8. Banking Due Diligence
Representation & Warranty — BAM BAM Due Diligence

Mannitoks represents and warrants that its banking partner(s) are not controlled by, affiliated with, or majority-owned by any of the families identified in BARSS Elite Extraction with Differential Targeting Model (EEDTM) research as extraction actors in Haiti. These families include, without limitation: Bigio, Apaid, Mevs, Brandt, Acra, and Madsen (collectively, the "Identified Extraction Actors").

Mannitoks shall provide SAKALA with documentation sufficient to verify this representation prior to the execution of any binding agreement. If this representation is found to be false or materially misleading at any time during the term of the partnership, SAKALA shall have the right to terminate immediately without notice or cure period, as further provided in Section 10.

For the avoidance of doubt, this clause exists because SAKALA is designed as an extraction-proof cooperative model. Routing cooperative member funds through institutions controlled by documented extraction actors would fundamentally compromise the integrity of the enterprise.

9. TETF Policy Alignment

Both Parties acknowledge their strategic alignment with the development of a Remittance Extraction Tax Framework (TETF), which would impose taxation on foreign money transfer operator fee revenue while exempting Haitian-owned platforms from such taxation.

Neither Party is obligated to lobby for, advocate for, or take any specific action in furtherance of TETF adoption. This section constitutes a statement of shared values and strategic alignment only.

10. Termination

Either Party may terminate this LOI upon ninety (90) days written notice to the other Party.

Notwithstanding the foregoing, SAKALA retains the right to immediate termination without notice in the event that the representation and warranty set forth in Section 8 is found to be false or materially misleading.

11. General Provisions

This LOI expresses the intent of the Parties to negotiate in good faith toward a binding agreement. Except for obligations of confidentiality implied herein, this LOI is non-binding. No Party shall be liable to the other for failure to reach a definitive agreement.
