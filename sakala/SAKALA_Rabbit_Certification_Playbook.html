Current Situation

SAKALA Site 0 has 30 rabbits in cages built from recycled tires and 25 youth who need to become certified rabbit producers and processors. No formal rabbit meat certification exists in Haiti. This playbook creates one... jointly branded with the country's largest rabbit network, backed by international veterinary expertise, and built on free training materials that already exist.

The Goal: Every youth graduates with a SAKALA × Makouti joint certificate, a tested 10-rabbit business plan, the skill to humanely slaughter and process rabbit meat, and the ability to train someone else. Total cost: $3,500–$7,500. USAID Farmer-to-Farmer can cover up to $5,000 of it.

Certification Program — 4 Modules, 4–6 Months

1
Breeding & Husbandry
4 weeks — Lead: Makouti trainer

Curriculum Sources

Makouti's existing producer curriculum + Peace Corps Handbook R0041 + FAO guides

Topics Covered

  • Breed selection — which breeds thrive in Haiti climate, sourcing stock
  • Housing & cage design — recycled-tire cage optimization, ventilation, cleaning protocols
  • Feeding & nutrition — moringa-based feeding program, forage identification, water systems
  • Breeding management — mating schedules, gestation tracking, kindling preparation, weaning
  • Health & disease prevention — common diseases, symptoms, prevention, when to isolate, basic treatment
  • Record keeping — breeding records, weight tracking, mortality logs, feed consumption

Practical Component

Each youth manages a minimum of 2 rabbits through at least 1 complete breeding cycle. Records are maintained in a standard logbook.

Week-by-Week

Week 1: Breed identification, anatomy, housing design. Build or improve one cage. Tour Makouti demo farm if possible.
Week 2: Feeding systems. Identify 10+ local forage plants. Set up moringa feeding schedule. Calculate feed costs.
Week 3: Breeding management. Pair rabbits. Begin gestation tracking. Set up record-keeping system.
Week 4: Health module. Disease identification. Quarantine protocols. Review and quiz.
2
Humane Slaughter & Butchering
3 weeks — Lead: F2F volunteer or Hares for Haiti

Curriculum Sources

Hares for Haiti field curriculum + Adam Danforth methods + FAO processing guide + Granja Jordán video course

Topics Covered

  • Humane handling — pre-slaughter care, stress reduction, ethical framework
  • Cervical dislocation — the primary method. Proper technique demonstrated and practiced
  • Skinning & evisceration — step-by-step, organ identification, hide preservation
  • Butchering into cuts — whole, halves, saddle, legs, offal. Yield optimization
  • Cold chain basics — temperature control in Haiti conditions, ice vs. salt methods
  • Hide & byproduct use — nothing wasted. Bones for stock. Hides for leather. Manure for compost
Critical: No youth processes a rabbit until they have observed at least 3 demonstrations and been individually assessed on technique. Humane slaughter is non-negotiable. Cervical dislocation must be clean and immediate. If a youth is not comfortable, they are not forced... they can pursue Track A (breeding) or Track C (education) instead.

Week-by-Week

Week 1: Theory and demonstration only. Watch Granja Jordán video. Observe 3+ live demonstrations by trainer. Anatomy review.
Week 2: Supervised practice. Each youth processes 1 rabbit under direct supervision. Technique assessed and corrected in real-time.
Week 3: Independent processing. Youth processes 1 rabbit with observation only (no hands-on correction). This is the certification skill test.
3
Meat Processing & Food Safety
2–3 weeks — Lead: Dr. Kaplan-Pasternak (remote) + on-site trainer

Curriculum Sources

HACCP basics (adapted for Haiti) + Dr. Kaplan-Pasternak protocols + Makouti market practices

Topics Covered

  • HACCP fundamentals — critical control points adapted for small-scale Haiti operations
  • Workspace sanitation — how to set up a clean processing area with available materials
  • Rabbit jerky production — the SAKALA Harvest Box product. Marination, drying, packaging
  • Packaging & labeling — portion control, weight standards, shelf-life management
  • Storage protocols — temperature management without reliable refrigeration
  • Water quality — testing, treatment, maintaining clean water for processing

Practical Component

Each youth must set up a complete sanitary workspace from scratch using available materials and pass an inspection. This is Certification Requirement #3.

4
Business Skills
2 weeks — Lead: SAKALA staff

Topics Covered

  • Unit economics — cost per rabbit (feed, housing, labor), price per kg, margin calculation
  • Market access — local markets, restaurant supply, Harvest Box channel, Makouti network
  • Haiti regulatory requirements — what exists (voluntary inspection), what doesn't (no formal rabbit cert), who to contact (Ministry of Agriculture, MSPP)
  • Record keeping for business — income/expense tracking, inventory, customer records
  • Cooperative economics — how SAKALA's model works, revenue sharing, reinvestment
  • Scaling plan — how a 10-rabbit unit grows to 50, then 100

Final Deliverable

Each youth writes a 10-rabbit business plan with 6-month sales projections, startup costs, break-even analysis, and identified market channels. This is Certification Requirement #4.

Certification Requirements — All 5 Must Pass

Each youth must complete all five requirements to receive the joint SAKALA × Makouti certificate. No shortcuts. No exceptions.

1
Knowledge Test — 40-question written exam covering breeding, nutrition, health, slaughter, food safety, and business. Pass: 80% (32/40). Two attempts allowed. Questions drawn from all four modules.
2
Skill Test — Humanely slaughter and fully butcher 1 rabbit. Assessed by trainer on: humane handling, clean cervical dislocation, proper skinning, correct cuts, zero waste. Pass/fail, no partial credit.
3
Food Safety Test — Set up a complete sanitary processing workspace from scratch. Assessed on: clean water source, sanitized surfaces, proper tool placement, waste separation, temperature control plan. Pass/fail.
4
Business Plan — Submit a written plan for a 10-rabbit production unit. Must include: startup costs, feed plan, breeding schedule, 6-month sales projections, identified market channels, break-even calculation. Reviewed by SAKALA staff.
5
Peer Mentorship — Successfully teach 1 concept from any module to another trainee (observed). Demonstrates mastery and builds SAKALA's train-the-trainer culture. Assessed by trainer on clarity and accuracy.
Certificate issued by: SAKALA × Makouti Agro Enterprise joint certificate. No government certification for rabbit production exists in Haiti. This joint certificate, backed by Makouti's network of 1,700+ producers and 327 rabbitries, is the strongest credential available. It establishes a de facto standard.

Advanced Tracks — After Certification

Certified youth can specialize. These are optional pathways that create differentiated roles within SAKALA's rabbit operation.

Track A

Master Breeder

Selective breeding for weight gain, litter size, disease resistance. Genetic record-keeping. Troubleshooting problem breeders. Goal: become the herd manager who improves stock quality across the entire operation.

Track B

Processing Specialist

Jerky production mastery. Value-added product development (pate, sausage, smoked). Branding and packaging design. Quality control for Harvest Box products. Goal: own the processing line.

Track C

Extension Educator

Train-the-trainer certification. Lead workshops for new cohorts and external farmers. Develop teaching materials in Kreyol. Partner with Makouti on outreach. Goal: scale SAKALA's training to other sites.

Track C is particularly important for SAKALA's franchise model. As new sites launch, they will need certified trainers who can replicate this program. The first cohort of 25 produces the trainers for the next 250.

Haiti Regulatory Reality

What exists

  • Rabbit meat inspection in Haiti is voluntary, not mandatory
  • Even under USDA rules (for export), rabbit inspection is voluntary
  • Haiti has no formal rabbit meat certification program
  • Makouti operates domestically and understands all local requirements

What to do

  • Contact Ministry of Agriculture (MARNDR) for any domestic requirements that may apply
  • Contact MSPP (Ministry of Public Health) for food safety requirements for processed meat products
  • Document all contacts and responses... if there are no requirements, document that too
  • The SAKALA × Makouti joint certificate creates a de facto industry standard where no de jure standard exists

Why this matters

The absence of regulation is an opportunity. SAKALA and Makouti can set the standard that others follow. When (not if) Haiti eventually formalizes rabbit production standards, SAKALA graduates will already exceed them.
