There is no structured certification for cooperative agriculture in Haiti. SAKALA changes that. At full network scale... 344 franchises across 7 departments, each placing 10 youth... the system produces 3,440 certified cooperative workers annually across 20 distinct certification pathways spanning 7 industries.

The macro numbers speak for themselves: $30.1M in annual GDP impact at full scale. 7,160 to 12,880 formal jobs created in a country where formal employment sits at 13%. Every certification is designed for international transferability... a SAKALA-trained youth can work in Jamaica, Trinidad, Guyana, or Suriname on the same credential. This is not job training. This is the first cooperative workforce pipeline in the Caribbean.

Household Income Generation

3,440 youth across 344 franchise atoms, earning $210-230 per month average. That is $86.7-94.9 million per year in household income increase flowing directly into Haiti's most underserved communities.

Each franchise atom costs $3,600 to launch and generates $25,164 in Year 1 revenue. That is a 699% return on investment in the first year alone. No aid program in Caribbean history has ever delivered that ratio.

The Multiplier Effect $12.04M direct GDP becomes $30.1M total when you apply the standard 2.5x multiplier for agricultural economies. Every dollar earned by a SAKALA cooperative member is spent locally... on food, transport, housing, school fees. The money circulates. It does not leave.

Part VI

What Universities Get

Eight concrete benefits. Zero ambiguity.
Benefit Detail
Published Research Real-world cooperative agriculture data from 344 sites across 7 departments. Multi-year longitudinal data. KoboToolbox feeds. Soil, crop, economic, social data. This is the largest agricultural research dataset in Haiti... and the university that partners first gets first authorship.
Student Placements 50+ field placement opportunities per year across 10+ active sites. Thesis research. Practicum. Extension practice. Students work with real cooperatives generating real revenue, not simulations.
Real-World Data Every franchise generates daily data: harvest logs, financial records, weather observations, quality inspections, soil measurements. 344 sites = the largest agricultural dataset in Haiti. Updated daily. Cleaned. Georeferenced.
Donor Credibility "Our university helped build a cooperative network that employs 3,440 youth across 7 departments." That sentence goes in every grant application, every accreditation report, every donor pitch. Documented, measurable, verifiable.
CARICOM Alignment CUCAER's mandate is reducing Caribbean food imports. SAKALA reduces food imports. Same goal, same beneficiaries, same reporting metrics. Partnership = automatic alignment with regional priorities.
New Program Development SAKALA's 20 certification pathways = 20 potential new certificate programs for the university catalog. Cooperative management. Tropical agriculture. Food safety. Essential oil distillation. Bamboo construction. Each one is a new enrollment stream.
Community Engagement Metrics Accreditation bodies increasingly require documented community engagement. SAKALA provides exactly that: ongoing, measurable, multi-year, multi-site. Not a one-day service project. A permanent institutional relationship with 344 community cooperatives.
Faculty Consulting Revenue $5,000-10,000 per year in consulting fees for 20 days of expert input. Faculty choose their own schedule. Applied work that generates both income and publications. Teaching load stays the same.
The Data Advantage Most agricultural research in Haiti relies on small samples, short timelines, and single sites. SAKALA offers 344 sites, multi-year data, and standardized collection protocols. For any agricultural researcher in the Caribbean, this is the dataset they have been waiting for. The first university to sign the MOU gets exclusive first-look access for the first 18 months.
Part VII

The Ask

Seven specific asks for universities. Two for CARICOM. Each one shows what flows in both directions.

For Universities

1

Curriculum Co-Development

Design certification modules jointly with SAKALA. Your faculty bring academic rigor. Our field teams bring operational reality. The output is a curriculum that actually works in the field.

University Provides
Curriculum design expertise, academic standards compliance, pedagogical frameworks, assessment rubrics, accreditation pathway
SAKALA Provides
Field-validated content, 344 active training sites, real-world case studies, cooperative management data, youth cohort for piloting
Output
20 certification modules ready for delivery. Each module = a new program in the university catalog and a new credential for SAKALA graduates.
Who Benefits
University (new programs, enrollment), SAKALA youth (recognized credentials), Haiti (skilled workforce), CARICOM (food security capacity)
2

Faculty Consulting

Experts teach specialized topics: soil science, cooperative law, food safety, essential oil chemistry, bamboo engineering. Twenty days per year. Faculty set their own schedule.

University Provides
Subject-matter experts (20 days/year), specialized technical knowledge, quality assurance, research supervision
SAKALA Provides
$5,000-10,000 annual consulting fees, field logistics, translation support, publication-ready data from consulting engagements
Output
3,440 youth trained by university-caliber faculty. Applied research papers. Faculty consulting revenue. Field experience for academic CVs.
Who Benefits
Faculty (income + publications), youth (expert training), university (community engagement credit), SAKALA (quality assurance)
3

Student Placements

Students conduct field work at SAKALA sites for thesis, practicum, and extension practice. Real cooperatives, real data, real communities.

University Provides
50+ students per year for field placements, thesis supervision, ethical review board clearance, academic credit frameworks
SAKALA Provides
10+ active field sites, housing coordination, community liaison, structured data collection protocols, KoboToolbox access
Output
50+ thesis projects per year. Longitudinal datasets. Trained graduates who understand cooperative agriculture from the inside.
Who Benefits
Students (field experience + thesis data), university (research output), SAKALA communities (technical assistance), Haiti (human capital)
4

Lab Access

Soil testing. Food safety analysis. Water quality monitoring. Essential oil composition. Bamboo structural testing. SAKALA needs labs. Universities have them.

University Provides
Laboratory facilities, analytical equipment, trained technicians, quality certification standards, ISO-compliant testing protocols
SAKALA Provides
344 sites of samples requiring analysis, testing fees, co-branded quality certification for cooperative products, publication data
Output
Certified product quality. Export-grade documentation. Soil health baselines across 7 departments. Published analytical data.
Who Benefits
SAKALA cooperatives (certified products), university labs (utilization + fees), consumers (quality assurance), export markets (compliance)
5

Research Partnerships

Co-author papers on cooperative agriculture, food security, youth employment, rural development, and agroecology. The data exists. It needs authors.

University Provides
Faculty co-authors, statistical expertise, journal access, peer review networks, grant writing capacity
SAKALA Provides
344-site longitudinal dataset, field access for primary research, community consent protocols, operational context for interpretation
Output
10-20 publications per year. The largest cooperative agriculture dataset in the Caribbean. Grant applications with built-in field sites.
Who Benefits
Faculty (publications + grants), university (research ranking), Caribbean agriculture (evidence base), SAKALA (validated methods)
6

Accreditation

Issue recognized certificates for SAKALA graduates. A credential that means something. A certification that opens doors.

University Provides
Accreditation authority, certificate design, academic standards verification, recognition within CARICOM education framework
SAKALA Provides
Trained cohorts ready for assessment, field portfolios documenting competencies, cooperative revenue data proving applied skill
Output
Recognized certifications for 3,440 youth. Portable credentials valid across CARICOM. A new certificate program in the university catalog.
Who Benefits
Youth (credential + mobility), university (enrollment + community impact), employers (verified skills), CARICOM (workforce quality)
7

Distance Delivery

Extend online programs to Haiti through existing platforms. UWI Open Campus already has the infrastructure. SAKALA has the students.

University Provides
Learning management system, online course delivery, remote assessment, digital credentials, Open Campus infrastructure
SAKALA Provides
3,440 enrolled learners, local facilitation hubs at cooperative sites, internet access points, community learning coordinators
Output
Distance education reaching Haiti's rural departments. University enrollment expansion. Blended learning model combining field practice with online theory.
Who Benefits
Youth (access to higher education), university (enrollment in underserved market), Haiti (educational infrastructure), UWI (mission fulfillment)

For CARICOM

A

Recognize SAKALA Certifications Through CUCAER Framework

CUCAER already has the mandate to coordinate agricultural education across the Caribbean. SAKALA certifications, co-developed with member universities, should be recognized within that framework. This is not a new bureaucratic structure. It is using the one that already exists.

CARICOM Provides
Regional recognition, CUCAER endorsement, cross-border credential portability, inclusion in Caribbean Vocational Qualification framework
Output
SAKALA graduates with credentials recognized across 15 CARICOM member states. Agricultural workforce mobility. Regional food security capacity.
B

Include SAKALA in Caribbean Food Security Strategy

The Caribbean imports $5 billion in food annually. CARICOM's food security strategy needs operational vehicles that actually reduce that number. SAKALA is one. $7.7-14.5 million in annual import substitution from a single program.

CARICOM Provides
Strategic inclusion, policy alignment, regional funding access, visibility at Heads of Government meetings
Output
SAKALA as a recognized vehicle within CARICOM food security architecture. Access to regional agricultural development funds. Policy coherence.
