Why Rabbits

Rabbit is the fastest land animal protein on earth. Gestation is 31–33 days. Slaughter weight is reached in 10–12 weeks. A single breeding unit of 6 does + 1 buck produces 28+ rabbits per month at steady state... 75 lbs of dressed meat every 30 days from a footprint smaller than a shipping container.

A breeding doe costs $3.50–$5.00 in Haiti. Total startup for a 6-doe operation is $170–$315. There is no cheaper protein production system on earth at this scale.

The Case for Haiti

  • 70–85% of feed is FREE. Moringa, sweet potato vines, leucaena, banana leaves... all grow wild or are already cultivated. Only 15–30% of feed needs to be purchased.
  • NO restaurants in Port-au-Prince or the diaspora currently serve rabbit. This is not a barrier... it is a greenfield market. Goat sells at $5.31–$8.72/lb in PaP. Chicken at $2.70–$3.40/lb. Rabbit slots at $4–$5/lb and outperforms both on feed conversion.
  • Makouti Agro Enterprise already operates 1,250 facilities with 32,650 breeding rabbits across ALL provinces. SAKALA does not need to build the national network... it already exists. Dan plugs in.
  • Only ONE rabbit jerky brand exists on earth for human consumption. Rickey's Jerky. At $7.33/oz ($117/lb). SAKALA enters a market with zero competition and infinite margin.
  • Rabbit is NOT subject to USDA mandatory inspection under the Federal Meat Inspection Act. Easier regulatory path than any other meat protein for US export.
  • Rabbit manure is the highest NPK of any livestock (2.4-1.4-0.6) and is "cold" manure... apply directly to soil without composting. Every rabbit is a fertilizer factory.

Regulatory Advantage

Rabbit is NOT subject to USDA mandatory inspection under the Federal Meat Inspection Act. Unlike beef, pork, chicken, and lamb, rabbit can be processed and sold without USDA inspector presence. This means:

  • No USDA slaughter facility certification required for small-scale processing
  • State-level regulation only (varies by state, generally permissive for small producers)
  • Cottage food laws may apply in many states for jerky products
  • Dramatically lower barrier to entry versus any other meat jerky

1. Fresh Dressed Rabbit

Local PaP market and restaurant sales. Slaughter, skin, eviscerate, vacuum seal. 3–7 day shelf life refrigerated. Primary revenue stream for local market. $4–$5/lb. Target: street food vendors, hotel restaurants, diaspora-owned restaurants doing "authentic" menus.

Heat Management

NON-NEGOTIABLE in Cité Soleil. Rabbits die at sustained temperatures above 32°C. Every element below is mandatory, not recommended. Skip any one and you lose animals.
  • Shade cloth over all hutches. No direct sun exposure at any hour of the day.
  • Fresh water 2x daily minimum. Dehydration kills faster than heat alone.
  • Ventilation holes in hutch design. Cross-ventilation on all sides. Wire mesh, not solid walls.
  • Breed for heat tolerance. Makouti local stock first (already proven). Baladi or TAMUK as alternatives. NZW only with full cooling infrastructure.
  • Never house in direct sun. Not for an hour. Not "temporarily." Never.
  • Night breeding. Rabbits breed more successfully in cooler temperatures. Schedule mating for evening or early morning.
  • Frozen water bottles in cages during extreme heat days. Rabbits press against them to cool down. Low-tech, effective.
