The Crisis

In 1950, Haiti was 50% forested. Today, less than 1% of primary forest remains. The single largest driver: charcoal production. 94% of Haitians cook with wood charcoal. 946,500 metric tons are produced annually. 53,000 hectares of trees are cut every year for charcoal... while only 2,000 hectares are replanted. The net loss: 51,000 hectares per year.

This is not a poverty problem. It is a $392 million market... the second-largest agricultural value chain in Haiti, contributing ~5% of GDP. Every household in Cité Soleil spends over 50% of income on cooking fuel. The demand is real, permanent, and growing. The supply is destroying the country.

The Solution Is Already Growing

Bambusa vulgaris arrived in Haiti in 1760. It has been naturalizing for 265 years. It grows wild along rivers, roadsides, and wastelands across the country... including the Port-au-Prince metropolitan area. It is a clumping species that regenerates after harvest. It produces the highest-quality charcoal of any tropical bamboo species tested.

Nobody in Haiti is harvesting it for charcoal.

Dual-Track Deployment

Track 1: Harvest Wild Stands (Immediate)

Bambusa vulgaris grows wild across Haiti. Map existing stands within 5-10km of Cité Soleil. Harvest mature culms (3+ years old, already in the ground for decades). Process into charcoal briquettes using simple drum kilns. Sell locally. Revenue starts in weeks, not years.

Track 2: Plant Site Grove (Parallel)

Plant Guadua angustifolia and Bambusa vulgaris cuttings directly into the contaminated soil at Site 0 (Cité Soleil HQ, former garbage dump). The bamboo serves three purposes simultaneously:

PurposeMechanismTimeline
Soil remediationBamboo absorbs lead, cadmium, arsenic, chromium from contaminated soil through roots. Harvesting removes metals from the site.Measurable improvement in 3-5 years. Significant cleanup in 10 years.
Charcoal productionNew culms harvestable for charcoal at Year 2-3. Regrow in 2-4 weeks after harvest. Sustainable annual harvest of 20-25% of grove.First site-grown charcoal by Year 2.
Construction materialGuadua angustifolia produces construction-grade poles at Year 3-5. Structural strength equivalent to steel in tension.Construction-grade by Year 3-5.
The soil remediation play: Right now, all food at Site 0 grows in raised beds and tire containers because the ground is a contaminated former dump. Bamboo planted directly in the toxic soil will absorb heavy metals through its root system over 5-10 years, progressively cleaning the ground. Eventually, this enables direct ground planting... expanding the site's productive capacity from ~1,500 m² of containers to the full 4,047 m² acre. That's a 2.7x expansion in growing area with zero additional land cost.
