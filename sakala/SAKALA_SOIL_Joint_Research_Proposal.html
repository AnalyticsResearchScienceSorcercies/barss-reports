Joint Crop Trial Proposal

SAKALA International × SOIL Haiti — Comparative Growing Media Research Program
Proposed by: Daniel Tillias, SAKALA International & Wesley Bertil, BARSS LLC
Proposed to: SOIL Haiti
Site: SAKALA Compound, Cité Soleil, Port-au-Prince
Duration: 6 months (extendable)
Date: April 2026

1. Executive Summary

SAKALA proposes a joint crop trial with SOIL Haiti comparing the effectiveness of SOIL Konpos Lakay at four concentration ratios across 8 crops, with parallel hydroponic control trials on selected crops. The research will be conducted by SAKALA franchise youth (ages 16+) at the SAKALA compound in Cité Soleil, with remote analytical support from BARSS LLC and academic oversight from UEH FAMV. All data will be shared with SOIL for product validation and marketing. Co-authorship on any resulting publications.

What SOIL Gets

  • Publishable data proving Konpos Lakay's effectiveness across 8 crop varieties at 4 concentration ratios
  • Marketing validation with side-by-side hydroponic comparison
  • Connection to SAKALA's 344-franchise network (future compost customers at scale)
  • IDB alignment (both organizations are HA-L1106 partners)

What SAKALA Gets

  • Free compost for the trial period
  • SOIL's brand credibility and technical expertise
  • Optimized crop/medium combinations for all future franchises
  • Research data for university partnerships and grant applications
  • Training for franchise youth in agricultural data collection

2. Research Design

Track A: Soil-Based Container Trials (SOIL Compost Evaluation)

For each crop, 4 treatments:

  • T1: 100% sand (control)
  • T2: 75% sand / 25% SOIL Konpos Lakay
  • T3: 50% sand / 50% SOIL Konpos Lakay
  • T4: 25% sand / 75% SOIL Konpos Lakay

3 replicates per treatment = 12 containers per crop.
8 crops × 12 containers = 96 containers total.

Track B: Hydroponic Control Trials (Selected Crops Only)

For crossover comparison, grow the same crops in hydroponic systems alongside soil containers:

Crop Hydroponic System Nutrient Solution Purpose
Pourpier Kratky (mason jar) Masterblend 4-18-38 Direct comparison: SOIL compost vs hydroponic yield
Peppers Drip system (bucket) Masterblend Direct comparison
Alfalfa Microgreen tray Masterblend Harvest as microgreens (7–14 day cycle)
Azolla DWC float (tray of water) No nutrients needed (fixes own nitrogen) Biomass production rate
Lettuce (bonus crop) Kratky + DWC Masterblend Benchmark crop (most studied hydroponic crop)

5 hydroponic crops × 3 replicates = 15 hydroponic units.

Total experimental units: 96 containers (Track A) + 15 hydroponic units (Track B) = 111 units.

Track C: Nursery Propagation (Concurrent, Separate Data)

Not part of the SOIL proposal directly, but runs on the same site:

  • Moringa: seed vs cutting comparison
  • Vetiver: slip division success rate
  • Breadfruit: root sucker propagation (TTFF method)
  • Pepper seedling production for Track A replanting

What youth CANNOT and DO NOT need to do:
  • Statistical analysis — Wesley/BARSS handles via KoboToolbox data export
  • Experimental design — already designed in this document
  • Publication writing — Wesley + university partner
  • Chemistry interpretation — LaMotte kit is color-matching, not chemistry

The youth are trained data collectors running a pre-designed protocol. The scientific rigor comes from the design and analysis, not the collection. This is standard practice in agricultural field research worldwide — trained community members collect data, researchers analyze it.

8. What SOIL Gets

SOIL provides approximately 500 kg of Konpos Lakay (valued at $0–75). In return, SOIL receives:

  1. Publishable data showing Konpos Lakay's effect on 8 crop varieties at 4 concentration ratios — data SOIL can use in marketing materials, grant applications, and partner presentations.
  2. Side-by-side comparison with hydroponic growing (Track B) — demonstrating where soil-based compost growing matches or exceeds high-tech methods, proving the value proposition for low-tech farming contexts.
  3. Connection to SAKALA's 344-franchise network — every franchise that deploys will use compost. At 344 franchises each using 500+ kg/year = 172,000+ kg/year of compost demand. This trial is the data that justifies that supply contract.
  4. Co-authorship on any resulting publications — shared credit in academic journals (target: JARTS, Tropical Agriculture UWI, PLOS ONE).
  5. IDB alignment — both SAKALA and SOIL are IDB partners (HA-L1106). Joint research strengthens both organizations' positions in the IDB ecosystem.
  6. Youth training pipeline — SAKALA franchise youth trained in composting through this trial become the workforce that scales SOIL's compost application across Haiti.

9. What SAKALA Gets

SAKALA invests approximately $521–653 in materials and provides the site, labor, and coordination. In return:

  1. Optimized crop/medium combinations for all 344 future franchises — the trial directly informs what goes in every raised bed.
  2. Hydroponic system validation — Track B data proves which crops are worth growing hydroponically in Haiti conditions, informing the Type 12 Hydroponics Training Center design.
  3. Youth trained in agricultural data collection — the 10 franchise youth who run this trial graduate with real research experience, forming the foundation of the SAKALA certification program.
  4. Academic credibility — co-authored publication with SOIL and UEH validates SAKALA's research capacity for future grant applications.
  5. SOIL partnership formalized — moves from informal relationship to documented research collaboration with shared data and mutual benefit.
