Section 5: [[DRIP]] Mechanism — The Multiplier

[!success] 20.9x With [[DRIP]] at 90% by Year 3, the Year 10 franchise count is 20.9x the count without DRIP. That is not a pitch number. That is a verified model output from V4 Gold DRIP.

What DRIP Is

Every quarter, each member receives a reward (Y1: $10.12, Y10 under Scenario F: $223.07). They have two choices:

  1. Cashout: Receive the reward as cash to their Boussoul / MoneyTalks / bank account
  2. DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment Plan): Auto-reinvest the reward into additional franchise deployment

DRIP is opt-in with a sensible default. The default is DRIP for Founders tier (15% of members) and cashout for Heritage tier (20% of members). Roots tier (65%) chooses at signup with a nudge toward DRIP. Members can flip the switch any quarter.

Modeled DRIP Rates

Year DRIP Rate Cashout Rate Why
1 75% 25% Members testing the model. High DRIP = trust + low reward amounts favor reinvest
2 73% 27% Slight drift as some members start harvesting
3 70% 30% DRIP stabilizes. Aggregate aggregate-level hits 90% of growth capital by this point
5 65% 35% Mid-cycle. BUILD phase starts.
10 55% 45% Mature network. HARVEST phase. Members consciously cashing out.

The headline “90% DRIP” in the V4 summary refers to DRIP as % of growth capital, not as % of members. By Year 3, 44.4% of all new franchise deployment comes from DRIP-reinvested rewards. By Year 7 it is 89.4%. By Year 10 it is 98.9%. Direct contributions become rounding error because the DRIP flywheel has taken over.

PART VI: THE HEDGES

Section 15: Risks + Mitigations

Risk 1: DRIP Rate Ramp Assumption Misses

Assumption: DRIP ramps from 75% (Y1) to 90% (Y3, measured as % of growth capital, not % of members).

Risk: Members prefer cashout more than modeled. If Y1 DRIP is 50% not 75%, the flywheel starts 18 months later.

Mitigation: - Default DRIP for Founders tier (15% of members) locks a floor - Reward tier structure: Higher DRIP percentages unlock bonus items in Heritage Box - Transparent dashboard: Every member sees their cumulative DRIP-enabled franchise share grow over time - The plan is still viable at 50% DRIP… Y10 is less dramatic but Y5 self-funding stands

Risk 2: Subscription Churn Exceeds 24%

Assumption: Y1 churn 24%, declining to 10% by Y10.

Risk: If real Y1 churn is 35%, Year 2 starts with 1,300 members not 1,520. This compounds.

Mitigation: - Quarterly Harvest Box itself is the anti-churn mechanism (people who got three good boxes do not unsubscribe) - Konbit pickup events (MA) create social retention - Church referral structure creates community-level retention (if the church stays in, its members stay in) - Konbit waiting list model Year 2+ replaces churned members with pre-committed queue

Risk 3: Haiti Operating Environment Deteriorates

Risk: Kidnapping, gang escalation, port closure, infrastructure collapse.

Mitigation: - Franchise geographic distribution (7 locations) hedges against single-site failure - Pignon and other northern sites have lower security risk than Port-au-Prince / Cité Soleil - Integrated stack generates all inputs on-site (no supply-chain dependency) - Processing + packaging happens at franchise; only finished boxes cross roads for export - Payroll runs on [[Boussoul]] / MoneyTalks fintech, not cash… survives banking shocks

Risk 4: Gold Price Shock

Risk: 12% appreciation assumption is base. If gold drops 20% in a year, reserve takes a hit.

Mitigation: - V4 model tested at 8% conservative and 18% aggressive scenarios. Both cases produce self-funding outcomes. - Gold is the trust anchor in Years 1-2, not the primary return driver. Less than 1% of Y10 reward pool comes from gold appreciation. Franchise surplus dominates. - If gold drops, Scenario F BUILD phase buys more ounces at lower prices, recovering the reserve on reversion

Risk 5: Fatraka Legal Structure Delays

Risk: Fatraka entity formation, dual-jurisdiction registration (Haiti + US), counsel onboarding takes longer than Q1 2026.

Mitigation: - SAKALA Community Center can fiscally sponsor Fatraka for up to 6 months if entity formation slips (firewall maintained through contractual structure while legal structure formalizes) - Q1 launch runs on subscription collection only (member acquisition) while franchise deployment waits for full Fatraka standup by end of Q2 - Parallel track: Fatraka legal, Fatraka banking, Fatraka insurance all run on separate tracks so the critical-path lag is the longest one, not the sum

Risk 6: Currency / Political Risk (Haitian Gourde)

Risk: Gourde devaluation eats franchise revenue when translated to USD for diaspora reporting.

Mitigation: - Franchises transact in gourde for local expenses, report in USD for diaspora dashboard - Gold reserve is priced in USD and held in fungible form, hedging gourde exposure - Wages are set in USD-equivalent (pegged to $4.50/day floor and rising), so workers are insulated from gourde weakness

APPENDIX B — WHY KONKRET WORKS (BARSS / EEDTM FRAMING)

[!info] This appendix is for funders, academic reviewers, and diaspora donors who want the underlying economics.

The extraction problem

Most Haiti aid flows through one or more intermediaries (international NGO, contractor, subcontractor, ground partner). At each layer, a fraction of the dollar is captured as overhead, administration, expatriate salary, or outright leakage. The BARSS EEDTM framework (Elite Extraction with Differential Targeting Model) has documented across 20 cases that elite capture rates (Theta, Θ) cluster around 0.80… meaning only ~20 cents of each dollar reaches the named beneficiary.

For Haiti specifically, post-earthquake aid analyses have repeatedly shown that the ground-level pass-through rate was far below 20%. This is not a scandal. It is a structural property of the intermediated aid model.

Why KONKRET inverts the math

KONKRET is structured so that the subscriber’s dollar passes through one accounting vehicle (Fatraka, firewalled from SAKALA Community Center) and reaches the ground franchise with minimal intermediation.

Why the subscription model beats the donation model

Donations fund a moment. Subscriptions fund a job. A young person hired under a 12-month subscription contract is employed for 12 months. A young person hired under a one-time donation is employed until the donation runs out… usually weeks.

Subscription income is also predictable. Predictable income allows the franchise supervisor to sign real contracts with real youth, which is what makes KONKRET a job program rather than a stipend program. At V4 Y1 scale (2,000 members × $360 = $720K), the predictability is structural, not aspirational.

4. The Dual Revenue Model

Pratik earns on three sides and pays out on one.

4a. Consumer Side (value in)

  • Free to join.
  • Users receive discounts at partner businesses (negotiated by Pratik, funded by the business as a customer-acquisition cost).
  • Users rate and review businesses using the five-criterion rubric.
  • Users direct impact: every qualifying purchase generates a contribution to the Pratik Impact Fund, and the user chooses which tracked project in Haiti receives it.
  • Users get a receipt of impact: a quarterly statement showing exactly where their contribution went, with photos, names, and numbers.

4b. Business Side (revenue in)

  • Listing fees (tiered: Basic / Featured / Premium).
  • Advertising revenue: businesses pay Pratik to produce Kreyòl-language ads targeted at the Haitian market. This is the line Dan has been watching happen around him for years and wants to bring in-house.
  • Transaction partnership fees: a small percentage of discount-driven transactions routed through Pratik.
  • Rating-linked promotion: higher-rated businesses get better placement. Businesses can improve their ranking by improving real-world performance, not by paying more.

4c. Impact Side (value out)

A published, audited allocation of Pratik net revenue flows to the Pratik Impact Fund, which capitalizes projects inside Haiti. Target allocation:

Allocation Share
Direct project funding (user-directed, flows to Haiti) 60%
Platform operations (Kreyòl CS, engineering, compliance) 25%
Reserve fund (multi-year runway, crisis buffer) 10%
Diaspora community grants (open RFP) 5%

The principle: the majority of net revenue leaves the platform and reaches Haiti-based projects, and users can see it leave. Per the V4 Financial Model, 60% of Pratik net revenue is the Impact Fund line… at Y1 scale ($142,500 gross), that lands the Impact Fund allocation at approximately $85,500 in Y1 (see §10).

5. Rating Criteria (Dan’s 5)

Every Pratik-listed business is rated on:

  1. Service performance — does the business deliver what it promises, on time?
  2. Quality of product — is the thing you bought actually good?
  3. Customer service — how are you treated, especially in Kreyòl?
  4. Amount supporting Haiti — what share of revenue or profit is routed to Haiti-based projects, suppliers, or employees?
  5. Number of Haitians hired — how many Haitians are on payroll, and in what roles?

Criteria 4 and 5 are the quiet revolution. Most review platforms only ask did they serve you well? Pratik also asks did they serve Haiti well? That turns every purchase into a small vote on the repatriation of value.

7. Why Pratik Is Extraction-Proof

Apply the [[EEDTM]] lens. Every funding structure has a capture rate, a Theta. The USAID-to-Haiti pipeline has a documented Theta in the 0.80-0.90 range: of every dollar appropriated, roughly 80 cents never reach a Haitian hand. Worse, the pipeline has a kill switch: a single administration changes policy and the whole thing stops. Dan’s son missed a year of school because of that kill switch.

Pratik is designed so that Theta cannot rise above a user-set ceiling, and so that no kill switch exists.

Extraction vector How Pratik blocks it
Gatekeeper (single funder can cut off the pipe) Revenue is distributed across hundreds of small business listings and thousands of consumer transactions. No single business or funder represents more than a small share of inflow.
Opaque allocation (funds enter a black box) Every user sees a line-item receipt of where their impact went. Projects are tracked live on the platform.
Intermediary capture (NGOs taking 60-80 cents per dollar) Published 60% allocation floor to Haiti projects. Audited. Reserve and ops capped. The majority leaves the platform and lands in Haiti.
Consumer indifference (nobody knows, nobody cares) Ratings include “amount supporting Haiti” and “Haitians hired” as scored criteria. Market pressure toward repatriation is built into the purchasing decision itself.
Political risk (one administration kills the program) Pratik has no dependency on any government budget line. It depends on diaspora purchasing behavior, which is one of the most stable flows on earth ($4B+/year from US alone).

[!success] Contrast with USAID model USAID Haiti: one funder, one political cycle, ~80% capture by US-side contractors, kill switch in Washington. When it stops, initiatives die and children miss school.

Pratik: hundreds of paying businesses, thousands of consumers, no political cycle, transparent allocation, no kill switch. When any one business leaves, the platform keeps running.
