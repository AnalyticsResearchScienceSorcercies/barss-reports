# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

## Purpose of This Report

This report provides an independent economic analysis of the costs and impacts associated with the termination of Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for Haitian nationals. The analysis was prepared at the request of the Institute for Justice & Democracy in Haiti (IJDH) in support of ongoing litigation challenging the TPS termination decision.

## Principal Findings

### Finding 1: Total Economic Disruption

The termination of Haiti TPS will result in total economic disruption of **$256.8 billion to $353.9 billion** over ten years using conservative methodology, or **$376.9 billion to $474.2 billion** using comprehensive industry multiplier methodology.

| Approach | 10-Year Impact (Low) | 10-Year Impact (High) |
|----------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Conservative (Direct Costs) | $256.8 billion | $353.9 billion |
| Comprehensive (With Multipliers) | $376.9 billion | $474.2 billion |

### Finding 2: Zero Documented Benefit

The federal government has produced no economic analysis documenting any fiscal or economic benefit from TPS termination. The cost-benefit ratio is undefined (division by zero).

### Finding 3: Healthcare System Crisis

TPS termination will remove **38,297 healthcare workers** from nursing homes and care facilities in four key states, placing **42 to 54 nursing homes** at risk of closure and displacing **5,250 to 7,030 elderly residents**.

### Finding 4: Extreme Differential Impact

The per-household cost to Haitian TPS families is **$1,999,942** over ten years, compared to **$1,058** for the average American household. Black (Haitian) households bear **20,110 times** the per-household burden of white households.

### Finding 5: Value Destruction Exceeds Capture

Using the Elite Extraction with Differential Targeting Model (EEDTM), we find that 55% of disrupted economic value is destroyed outright, not transferred to any party. For every dollar potentially captured by enforcement contractors, approximately $22 is destroyed.

# QUALIFICATIONS AND METHODOLOGY

## Organizational Background

BARSS LLC (Bertil's Analytics Research Sciences & Sorceries) is a forensic research organization specializing in quantitative economic analysis of extraction mechanisms. BARSS has developed and validated the Elite Extraction with Differential Targeting Model (EEDTM) across 20 historical cases spanning 200 years, including colonial extraction, labor exploitation, financial crises, and policy-induced economic disruptions.

The Reparations Finance Lab is a research initiative focused on quantifying historical and ongoing economic harms to marginalized populations.

## Methodological Framework

This analysis employs three complementary methodological approaches:

### 1. Direct Cost Aggregation (ILRC Method)

Following the methodology established by the Immigrant Legal Resource Center in their 2017 analysis, we calculate direct costs including:
- Federal enforcement expenditures
- Lost wages and economic output
- Tax revenue foregone
- Replacement and turnover costs

### 2. Industry Multiplier Analysis (CAP Method)

Following the methodology employed by the Center for American Progress, we apply Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) Regional Input-Output Modeling System (RIMS II) multipliers to capture indirect and induced economic effects.

### 3. EEDTM Destruction Analysis

We apply the Elite Extraction with Differential Targeting Model to quantify value destruction—economic losses that are not transferred to any party but are annihilated through the crisis mechanism.

# BACKGROUND AND SCOPE

## Temporary Protected Status Overview

Temporary Protected Status (TPS) is a humanitarian immigration designation established under the Immigration Act of 1990 (8 U.S.C. § 1254a). TPS provides temporary lawful status to foreign nationals from countries affected by ongoing armed conflict, environmental disasters, or other extraordinary conditions.

## Haiti TPS Designation History

| Date | Action | Basis |
|------|--------|-------|
| January 21, 2010 | Initial Designation | 7.0 magnitude earthquake |
| Various 2010-2021 | Multiple Extensions | Ongoing crisis |
| November 2017 | Termination Announced | Trump Administration |
| 2018-2024 | Litigation Stays | Court orders |
| January 2025 | TPS Expires | Administration decision |

## Scope of This Analysis

This analysis quantifies the economic impacts of terminating TPS for the 330,735 Haitian nationals currently holding TPS status. The analysis covers:

1. Direct federal costs of enforcement
2. Lost economic output and tax revenue
3. Sectoral disruptions (healthcare)
4. International economic impacts (Haiti)
5. Derivative impacts on US citizens
6. Systemic costs (housing, courts, employers)
7. Value destruction through crisis mechanism

The analysis period is ten years, with present values calculated using a 4% discount rate consistent with federal cost-benefit analysis standards.
