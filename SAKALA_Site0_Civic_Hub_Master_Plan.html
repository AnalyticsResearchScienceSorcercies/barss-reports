Part I

The Pivot: Education → Civic Infrastructure

Education produces graduates. Civic infrastructure produces an economy.

The Problem with Education Alone

Site 0 was built as an agricultural education center. It works. Youth learn to grow moringa, raise rabbits, process charcoal, manage raised beds. They take classes. They use the library. They play ball on the court. But then what?

A trained youth with no economy to participate in is a trained youth with no income. Agriculture education without market access is a hobby. Financial literacy without financial infrastructure is theory. Every NGO on the island runs education programs. Almost none of them build the economic plumbing that makes education worth anything.

That is the gap. And it is the gap that Site 0 is now built to close.

The insight: Site 0 is not a school. It is the kernel of a self-sustaining local economy. The basketball court, the library, the classrooms... these are not amenities. They are the onboarding funnel. Every community touchpoint is a conversion opportunity. Every person who walks through the gate for a book or a basketball game leaves as a potential participant in a cashless economic loop that did not exist before they arrived.

What "Civic" Means Here

Civic infrastructure is the set of systems that allow a community to function as an economy. Payment rails. Identity cards. Transaction data. Merchant networks. Cooperative governance. Health data collection. Financial literacy that connects to an actual financial instrument in your hand.

Haiti is approximately 95%+ cash economy. Cité Soleil has virtually zero POS or NFC infrastructure. No card readers. No digital payment acceptance. No transaction records. No data. No proof that economic activity even happens. To the World Bank, Cité Soleil's internal economy is invisible.

SAKALA becomes the entity that builds the payment rails. Not because we are a fintech company... we are not. Because we already have the foot traffic, the trust, the physical spaces, and the partner (Mannitoks) with the technology. The missing piece was never the tech. It was the distribution channel. Site 0 is the distribution channel.

The formula: Community spaces generate foot traffic. Foot traffic enables enrollment. Enrollment creates cardholders. Cardholders need merchants. Merchants need cardholders. The loop closes. The economy turns on.

Part III

The Enrollment Machine

Site 0 becomes a card and identity enrollment factory.

Permanent Enrollment Station

One corner of the library becomes a permanent enrollment desk. Laptop, fingerprint scanner, camera, card printer. Operational during all library and class hours: 9am to 5pm, six days a week. Walk in for a book, walk out with a working NFC smart card and an activated Mannitoks wallet.

This is not a special event. This is a standing civic service, like a post office or a DMV, except it takes 15 minutes instead of 4 hours and it is free.

The 15-Minute Flow

Walk In → ID Check → Photo → Fingerprint → Card Printed → Wallet Activated → First Balance Loaded → Walk Out With Working NFC Card

The Enrollment Cascade

Enrollment does not happen all at once. It cascades outward in concentric rings from the people who already trust Site 0 the most.

  • Ring 1 (Month 1-2): 100 active youth. They get cards first. They are the proof of concept. They use the cards at the court, the library, the processing hub. They show their families.
  • Ring 2 (Month 3-4): Families of youth. Mothers, fathers, siblings. 200-300 additional cards. The enrollment pitch is simple: your child already has one. It works. You want one.
  • Ring 3 (Month 5-6): Community at large. The monthly Harvest Day on the basketball court becomes a mass enrollment event. Music. Food. Card sign-up tables. Target: 500 total cards by Month 6.
  • Ring 4 (Month 7+): Merchants. By now the merchant play is obvious... 500 people have cards. The merchants come to us.

Mass enrollment events happen monthly on the basketball court, timed to Harvest Day celebrations. The court holds 200+ people. Music plays. Food is served. Youth demonstrate how the card works. Enrollment tables line the sidelines. One event. Fifty new cardholders. The court is doing more economic development in one afternoon than most NGO programs accomplish in a fiscal quarter.

Part IV

The Merchant Play: Free Cashless Adoption

Cité Soleil has virtually zero digital payment infrastructure. SAKALA provides it free.

The Opportunity

There are no card readers in Cité Soleil. No POS terminals. No NFC acceptance points. Every transaction happens in cash. Cash that gets stolen, lost, counterfeited, and... most importantly... generates zero data. The formal economy has no idea what happens in Cité Soleil because the transaction layer is invisible.

SAKALA does not need to compete with existing payment infrastructure. There is none. We are not disrupting an incumbent. We are creating a category. And we are giving it away for free.

The Pitch to Merchants

"Accept cards. Get more customers. Zero monthly fees. We give you the reader."

  • Free NFC reader to any merchant within 2km of Site 0 (cost: $50-150 per unit, SAKALA subsidizes)
  • Merchant signs up for a Mannitoks merchant account (free)
  • Merchant can now accept: NFC card tap, MonCash, NatCash, QR code scan
  • Zero monthly fees. Zero transaction fees on closed-loop payments
  • Merchant gets access to MACHE wholesale marketplace for restocking

50 Merchants, Phase 1

Target: 50 merchants within the first 4 months. These are not random businesses. They are the places our 100 cardholding youth already spend money.

1 Food vendors near Site 0
2 Madan Sara (market women)
3 Phone credit / airtime sellers
4 Moto-taxi operators
5 Pharmacies / health shops
6 Building supply shops
7 Clothing / fabric vendors

Each merchant onboarded equals one more place the NFC card works. Each new acceptance point makes the card more valuable. Each card that gets more valuable makes the next merchant easier to recruit. This is the network effect. It compounds.

The math: 100 youth cardholders spending an average of 500 HTG/week each = 50,000 HTG/week in potential digital transactions. That is real revenue for merchants who currently have zero digital payment capability. The reader pays for itself in the first month. Every month after that is pure upside.

Part V

The Closed Loop Economy

Value that stays in the loop is value that cannot be extracted.

The Circular Flow

The entire design converges on one objective: keep value circulating inside Cité Soleil instead of leaking out to middlemen, cash-exchange operators, and extractive supply chains. Every transaction that stays in the loop is a transaction where Phi equals zero. No upstream cut. No financier's share. No extraction.

Franchise revenue flows directly to youth wallets. No cash handling. No intermediary.

Product sales fund the next cycle of production and youth wages.
6

The loop closes. Value stays in Cité Soleil. Phi = 0.000.
↑  LOOP CLOSES  ↑

The Key Metric: Loop Retention Rate

What percentage of value stays in the loop versus leaking out to cash? That is the number that matters. Cash is the leak. Every cash-out is extraction. Every time someone converts their card balance to paper gourdes to pay a merchant who does not accept cards, value escapes the system.

The solution is not to ban cash-outs. The solution is to make cash-outs unnecessary by ensuring the card works everywhere the cardholder needs to spend.

Target: 80% closed loop within 6 months of 100-card deployment.

Every transaction in the loop = zero extraction. Phi = 0.000.

Compare that to USAID's Haiti spending: $3.085B disbursed, Phi = 0.987. For every dollar USAID sends, 98.7 cents goes to American contractors, Beltway consultants, and overhead. 1.3 cents reaches Haitians.

We are building the opposite. An economy where the extraction constant is zero.

What the Loop Funds

This is not abstract. The closed loop economy funds concrete things that are already operating at Site 0:

  • Moringa powder production (superfood, export potential, high margin)
  • BonZeb sustainable charcoal (alternative to deforestation charcoal)
  • Rabbit protein production (low input, high output, fast cycle)
  • Raised bed vegetables (food security + market sales)
  • Bamboo perimeter maintenance (erosion control + building material)

The gardens are not a teaching tool. They are the supply chain. The NFC cards are not a technology demo. They are the payment layer. The merchants are not partners. They are the economy. Site 0 is the operating system that connects all of it.

The Merchant Onboarding Assistant

The last row is the critical one. Youth do not just receive training. They become the training team. A 19-year-old who has used an NFC card for three months, who understands how the Mannitoks wallet works, who can troubleshoot a failed tap... that person is the most effective merchant onboarding agent SAKALA could deploy.

They go door to door in Cité Soleil. They carry a demo NFC reader and a stack of merchant sign-up forms. They speak Kreyol. They are from the neighborhood. They are trusted. And they are paid for every merchant they successfully onboard.

This is a job AND it builds the network. Youth employment is not a separate program from merchant acquisition. It is the same program. Every youth who onboards a merchant earns a stipend, gains a marketable skill, and strengthens the closed loop by one more acceptance point. The incentives are perfectly aligned.

Curriculum Integration

Nothing is removed. Everything is extended. Agriculture class still teaches soil composition and crop rotation. But now the last 20 minutes of every class session connects the lesson to the economic pipeline: this moringa goes to the processing hub, gets packaged, gets listed on MACHE, gets purchased by a merchant using their Mannitoks balance, and the revenue goes to your card. You grew this. You get paid for this. That connection... from seed to card balance... is the education that matters.

Part VII

Data as Proof

Every card tap generates evidence. Evidence that this works.

What Gets Measured

A cash economy produces no records. A card economy produces a dataset. Every single NFC tap at a merchant terminal is a data point. Aggregated, anonymized, and visualized, these data points become the most powerful grant application material in Haiti development history.

  • Transaction volume: Proof of economic activity where the World Bank currently sees none
  • Merchant adoption rate: Proof of infrastructure growth, week over week
  • Closed-loop percentage: Proof of extraction elimination (what % stays in the system)
  • Youth spending patterns: Proof of economic behavior change (savings rate, investment in inventory, merchant activity)
  • Geographic heat maps: Where the card is used most, revealing actual economic corridors in Cité Soleil
  • Velocity of money: How fast does a gourde circulate inside the loop before leaking to cash?

Who Gets the Data

This data feeds into quarterly reports for every funder category that matters:

  • World Bank / IDB: "We built measurable financial inclusion infrastructure. Here are the numbers."
  • Ford Foundation / Open Society: "We eliminated extraction in a closed economic loop. Here is Phi going to zero."
  • USAID (ironically): "We achieved what your $3.085B did not. Here is the per-dollar comparison."
  • Academic partners: Craemer, Darity, Kaboub get real-time EEDTM validation data

Real-Time Transparency Dashboard

A public-facing dashboard (Datawrapper + Mannitoks API) shows:

  • Total cards issued (live counter)
  • Total merchants active (live counter)
  • Transaction volume this month (aggregated, anonymized)
  • Closed-loop retention rate (the headline number)
  • On-chain USDC reserve balance (for funder transparency)

"We didn't just give them money. We gave them an economy. Here's the data."

That is the one-sentence pitch to every funder meeting. The dashboard URL is the second sentence. The quarterly PDF with charts is the follow-up email. The data does the talking.
