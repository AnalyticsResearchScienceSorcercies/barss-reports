Section III

The Distribution Model: "The Virus"

The card spreads because it is useful. Not because anyone is pushing it. Not because there's a marketing budget. Not because an NGO is handing them out at a tent. Books pull people in. Payments keep them using it. Word of mouth does the rest.

Week 1-4
200 cards 50 library visitors per week. Each one gets a card. They came for books. They left with infrastructure.
Month 2
600 cards Each cardholder tells 3 family members. "Get your library card... you can use it to pay at the market."
200 × 3 = 600 family members come in for their own cards
Month 3
1,000+ cards Word of mouth compounds. Neighbors see neighbors tapping cards at merchants. "Where did you get that?" "The library."
Month 4
Merchants start asking The demand flips. Merchants come to Site 0: "How do I accept those cards?" They see their customers paying with them at other stalls. They want in. SAKALA gives them a free NFC reader.
Month 6
2,000+ cards The tipping point. Enough density that the card becomes the default. Cash doesn't disappear... it just becomes less convenient than tapping.

The network effect is the engine. More cardholders means more merchants want in. More merchants means the card is more useful. More useful means more people want one. The library is the ignition switch. Everything after that is thermodynamics.

Section IV

The Book Strategy

The library has to be a real library. A good one. The books are not a prop. If the library is mediocre, people won't come back. If people don't come back, the card doesn't spread. The books are the engine.

High-Demand Collection

Kreyol children's books. Haitian history. Practical skills... carpentry, electrical, agriculture, cooking. Health guides. Literacy workbooks. Stock what people actually need and actually want to read.

Rotating Collection

New books every month. Keeps people coming back. Each visit means another tap on the card when checking out. Frequency of use drives habit formation. Habit drives adoption.

Children's Programs

Reading programs for kids. Story time. Homework help. Their parents need a card too. One child enrolled means one or two adult cards issued. Children are the multiplier.

Book Clubs

Community gathering. Discussion groups. Literacy circles. More sign-ups, more engagement, more reasons to walk through the door every week.

Publisher Partnerships

Partner with Haitian publishers for Kreyol-first titles. Uncle Bobbie's bookstore for diaspora connection. LibraryThing for digital catalog management. Build the supply chain for Haitian literature.

Self-Sustaining Model

A small percentage of card transaction fees funds new book acquisitions. Community members can donate books by depositing value on their card: 1 book donated = 25 HTG credit. The library funds itself through the economy it created.

Section VI

Enrollment Flow

Ten steps. Fifteen minutes. Zero cost to the person. They came for a book. They leave with financial infrastructure. Every step is simple. Every step is human. No jargon. No forms in triplicate. No "terms and conditions."

1

Walk In

Person enters library. Sees books on shelves. Sees other people reading. Sees kids doing homework. Sees a few people tapping cards at the desk. Normal.

2

Ask

"I'd like to borrow a book." Staff smiles. "Of course. You just need a library card. It's free. Takes about 15 minutes."

3

Register

Name. Address. Phone number if they have one. Date of birth. Simple form on a tablet. No government ID required. No proof of address required.

4

Photo

Webcam captures photo. Stored locally and encrypted. This becomes the photo on the card and the photo in the Mannitoks system. One click.

5

Biometric

Fingerprint scan. Optional but encouraged. "This is so we can recover your card if you ever lose it." Simple. Practical. No coercion.

6

Print

Card printed on-site. PVC card printer. Takes 3-5 minutes. The person watches their name and photo appear on a real, professional card.

7

Activate

Staff taps the new card on the NFC reader. Wallet created on Mannitoks. Account linked. The card is live.

8

Welcome Bonus

50 HTG loaded onto the card. "This is a gift from the library." The person now has money on a card they got for free. Immediate value. Immediate trust.

9

Tutorial

Two-minute walkthrough. "Tap here to pay at a merchant. Check your balance here. Borrow books here. That's it." No app required. No download. The card works.

10

Checkout

Person borrows their first book. Taps their card at the desk. Walks out the door with a book in one hand and a functional payment card in the other.

15 minutes. Zero cost to the person. They came for a book. They left with a payment card, a digital wallet, a community ID, and cooperative membership. They didn't apply for anything. They didn't qualify for anything. They got a library card.

Φ (Phi) measures the fraction of transaction value extracted from the community. A conventional bank or remittance service operates at Φ = 0.03-0.08. Section X

Why It Works

Seven properties. Each one is load-bearing. Remove any one and the system still works, but slower. Together they create something that spreads on its own.

Low Barrier

Everyone wants a library card. No income requirement. No credit check. No government ID. No minimum balance. You walk in, you get one. The lowest possible friction to entry.

Immediate Value

Books to borrow right now. 50 HTG on the card right now. Not a promise of future benefits. Not "your application is being processed." Value in your hand before you walk out the door.

Daily Utility

Payment at merchants. Balance checking. Book borrowing. This is not a card you use once and forget. This is a card you tap every day. Daily use builds daily habit.

Social Proof

"Everyone at the library has one." "My neighbor uses it at the market." The card becomes visible in daily life. Seeing others use it is the most powerful marketing that exists.

Zero Stigma

This is not a "poor person's card." Not an "aid recipient ID." Not a "beneficiary voucher." It's a library card. The most innocent civic object in existence. Nobody is embarrassed to carry a library card.

Viral by Design

Utility drives word of mouth. Not advertising. Not outreach workers. Not flyers. "I got this card at the library. I can pay with it at the market. You should get one." That's it. That's the entire marketing strategy.

Self-Sustaining

Transaction fees fund more books. More books bring more visitors. More visitors get more cards. More cards drive more transactions. The loop closes. The library feeds the economy feeds the library.

The most powerful piece of financial infrastructure in Cité Soleil is going to be a library card.

Nobody will see it coming. That's the point.
