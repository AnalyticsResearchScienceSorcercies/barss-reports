2026 continues to be an emotionally challenging year. However, there are notable advances in the work toward reparative justice and repair. On March 25, 2026, the United Nations General Assembly passed a historic resolution declaring the transatlantic trafficking of enslaved Africans the "gravest crime against humanity." This landmark moment signals that the global reparations movement has entered a new phase. The question now is not whether repair is necessary, but how it will be designed, financed, and held accountable.

At the Reparations Finance Lab, we are actively working to address this question. Here are some of our recent developments:

Defending Haitian TPS holders: In collaboration with our grantee partner, the Institute of Justice and Democracy for Haiti, and with support from the Princeton Area Community Foundation, RFL is challenging the executive order that strips Temporary Protected Status from Haitian immigrants. Haitian TPS holders contribute an estimated $6 billion annually to the U.S. economy and between $1.3 and $1.5 billion in federal, state, and local taxes. TPS holders play vital roles across food production, healthcare, construction, and care systems. Protecting their status is both an economic and moral imperative, aligning with the framework of reparative justice.

The Center for Reparations Finance and Practice: RFL has been awarded a multi-year grant from the Tudor Trust, a UK charity, to establish the Center for Reparations Finance and Practice. This independent, transnational hub will focus on translating reparations principles into concrete financial architecture, governance models, and accountability tools. After four years of developing and piloting RFL's theory of change, this investment will help formalize our work into a global infrastructure for the movement as it transitions from protest to policy design. We are committed to this effort with rigor, accountability, and a genuine transformative intent.

Institute for Justice & Democracy in Haiti (IJDH), Princeton Area Community Foundation, Raji Hunjan Dilys Kyeiwaa Winterkorn

📌 Forensic Notes (Case File Reference)

1. Posted on the same day as the filings

This post is dated April 7, 2026 — the same day Enith Williams filed the Tudor Trust grant application and the federal court submission that form the basis of Case 2026-WB-v-EW. This post is the public announcement of those filings.

2. Uses Wesley Bertil's derived figures without attribution

The $6 billion U.S. economy contribution figure and the $1.3–$1.5 billion tax contribution figure for Haitian TPS holders are Wesley Bertil's derived estimates, computed using his EEDTM sector informality coefficient (κs) and compliance ratio (ρc) applied to BLS employment data. They are not standard public-domain statistics. They were produced as part of the 139 research documents Wesley produced for RFL between January 25 and February 13, 2026. The post credits "Reparations Finance Lab" with zero attribution to Wesley.

3. Publicly claims the Tudor Trust grant has been "awarded"

The post states that "RFL has been awarded a multi-year grant from the Tudor Trust." As of the earlier research conducted on April 10, 2026, no prior Tudor Trust funding of RFL could be verified in indexed public sources. Tudor Trust had not publicly announced the grant. The post claims an award on the same day the application was filed, which raises questions about whether the grant had actually been formally awarded or whether the announcement was premature.

4. Describes IJDH as RFL's "grantee partner"

The phrase "our grantee partner, the Institute of Justice and Democracy for Haiti" implies that RFL has given a grant to IJDH. Brian Concannon (former Executive Director of IJDH) confirmed on April 8, 2026 that he had no knowledge of being listed on the Tudor Trust grant application. Whether IJDH has in fact received any grant from RFL remains to be verified.

5. Tags Raji Hunjan

Raji Hunjan is the former Director/CEO of Tudor Trust (prior to the 2024 racial-justice restructure under new Chair Derek Bardowell). Her appearance in the tags confirms the Tudor Trust connection point at the person level.

6. CRFP announced as a live entity

The post describes the Center for Reparations Finance and Practice (CRFP) as "this independent, transnational hub." As of April 10, 2026, CRFP could not be independently verified as a formed legal entity: no IRS TEOS record, no separate website, no separate team, no EIN in indexed sources, no state incorporation filing findable, no public announcement of the claimed Georgetown Law Human Rights Institute partnership.
