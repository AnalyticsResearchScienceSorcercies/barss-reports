I. Introduction and Core Finding

The United States is experiencing a modest but persistent rise in food prices at the same time that it is losing foreign-born workers — including Haitian TPS holders — who are central to agriculture, meatpacking, and food service. Economic research and federal agency warnings show that when immigrant labor in these sectors is reduced, food production is disrupted and prices rise.

PROSE The "central to agriculture, meatpacking, and food service" framing is the through-line of the BARSS sector portfolio. SRC: TPS_Industry_Shock_Response_Analysis.md — "TPS termination would remove 163,000+ workers from 7 critical sectors simultaneously" (Wesley Bertil, BARSS vault).

The analysis that follows also addresses two additional dimensions of harm. First, the termination of TPS will fall disproportionately on U.S.-citizen children and low-income households already under financial strain. Second, restrictions on remittances — a closely related policy risk — would amplify the damage to Haiti's economy and its residents, compounding the effects of TPS termination at both ends of the migration corridor.

II. What Is Happening to Prices Now

Federal data show that food prices are still rising faster than pre-pandemic levels and faster than in some other consumer categories. Over the 12 months ending in December 2025, consumer prices rose about 2.7 percent, while food prices rose 3.1 percent, with food at home up 2.4 percent and food away from home up 4.1 percent.

CURATED BLS CPI series — these are BLS's own headline numbers. Wesley curated them across multiple BARSS price-impact documents. USDA's Food Price Outlook indicates that food prices in 2025 were roughly 3.0 percent higher than in 2024... For a typical household spending about $165 per week on groceries, a 3 percent increase in food prices translates into roughly $250–$300 in additional annual costs.

Haitian TPS holders are not a marginal workforce; they are embedded across U.S. food and care systems. The Haitian Bridge Alliance and FWD.us estimated in early 2026 that approximately 200,000 or more Haitian TPS holders are currently working in the United States — a figure that may undercount the actual workforce, given the American Immigration Council's finding of a 95 percent employment rate among TPS holders.

Documentation compiled in early 2026 finds that Haitian TPS holders contribute approximately $6 billion annually to the U.S. economy, and between $1.3 and $1.5 billion in combined federal, state, and local taxes each year.
