TapTap Franchise Farm

Manager Manual and Worked Example. Rebuilt 17 August 2026 from the module folders, after the Komite Jaden was removed and every open correction applied. One committee, one election: the manager runs the day, the Komite Vijilans watches the money, and the assembly is all ten.

HOW THIS WORKS

Six modules. Twelve hours, split across three days, or across two weekends if the trainee
is working.

Every module ends with one thing the trainee has to DO, not recite. The exam at the end is
those same things done in front of an assessor, in one sitting.

Training is paid at 2 dollars per hour. The hours are counted from day one and paid into
the first payroll. A trainee who starts and does not finish is not paid. This is said out
loud on day one. Job Power is a program that teaches young people to deliver and to see
the result in real money.

WHAT IS EXPECTED OF YOU

Give this page to the trainee on day one. They keep it. Nothing on it is a surprise.

You are being trained to run a site. There are three levels. You know exactly what each one
requires before you start, and nobody moves you up or holds you back for any reason that is
not written here.

LEVEL 1 — TRAINEE

You are learning. Your hours are counted from the first one and paid into your first payroll
when you finish.

  [ ] Attend all six modules
  [ ] Fill one recruitment priority list
  [ ] Run one youth conversation and one parent conversation, watched
  [ ] Facilitate one training session for the apprentices
  [ ] Fill one complete weekly planning sheet, all six lines
  [ ] Fill one list of the most suitable growing product or service for the community
  [ ] Find one real problem on a site
  [ ] Send three days of daily reports that are accurate
  [ ] Run one Friday circle, including asking the Vijilans out loud
  [ ] Run one community leader conversation
  [ ] Pitch one host partner, one sponsor and one buyer
  [ ] Write one Facebook post using a real number
  [ ] Say the five rules from memory, in Kreyòl

LEVEL 2 — CERTIFIED

You have passed the exam. You hold the credential whether or not a site is open yet.

  [ ] Passed all six exam tasks with an assessor who did not recruit you
  [ ] Your name is on the public list of certified managers
  [ ] You hold the one-page summary in quantity to hand out
  [ ] You are first in line for the next site that opens

LEVEL 3 — RUNNING A SITE

You have a site. This is what the site is judged on, and therefore what you are judged on.

  [ ] The daily report goes in every day, and the numbers are true
  [ ] The best photo of the day is captured
  [ ] A good post is published on social media
  [ ] The weekly planning sheet is on the wall every Monday
  [ ] Friday circle happens every week and the Vijilans is asked every week
  [ ] The election happened and the Komite Vijilans exists
  [ ] The monthly WhatsApp summary went out
  [ ] Every young person on the site was paid what they were owed, on time
  [ ] Nothing was promised to a buyer that was not already harvested

WHAT WILL NEVER BE HELD AGAINST YOU

  - A bad harvest you reported honestly
  - Saying you do not know a number instead of guessing it
  - A Vijilans freeze on your site
  - A problem you raised early

WHAT ENDS IT

  - Reporting a number you did not measure
  - Paying yourself or anyone else before the group has read the numbers out loud
  - Running Friday circle without asking the Vijilans
  - Promising a family a total earning figure
  - Giving a place to a relative ahead of someone higher on the priority list

MODULE 1 — RECRUITMENT

How you find ten young people, how you choose which ten, and how you keep them

WHAT THE MANAGER DOES

1. Two conversations, never one. You sit with the young person. Then you sit with a parent
   or the adult responsible for them. Both happen before anyone signs anything.

2. The youth conversation is about the work and the money. The parent conversation is about
   safety, hours, and what the family gains. They are different conversations and you do not
   run one script for both.

3. You leave paper behind. Every conversation ends with the one-page summary in the family's
   hands, and the link written on it, so they can look again after you leave and show it to
   someone else.

4. You recruit more than you need. If the site takes ten, you train fourteen. Everyone who
   completes training holds a TapTap credential whether or not they get the slot, and they
   are first in line when the next site opens. This is how you stop scrambling when someone
   drops out.

WHO IS A PRIORITY

You will have more people who want a place than places. How you choose is the first thing
the community will judge you on, so you write it down before you start choosing.

Rank in this order:

  1. Out of school, and out because of money rather than choice
  2. Already earning for the household, or the only person in it who could
  3. No adult in the household currently earning
  4. Lives close enough to walk to the site
  5. Referred by the host partner or a community leader
  6. Says yes to the full training and to the daily reporting rhythm

A referral moves someone up the list. It does not put them at the top, and it does not
guarantee a place. Say that to the person who made the referral, at the time they make it,
not later when you have to explain yourself.

THREE HARD RULES

  - No relative of the manager or of a Komite Vijilans member in the first cohort.
  - The list is written and posted where people can see it. Not kept in your head.
  - If you skip someone, the reason is written next to their name.

The list is not paperwork. It is your protection. Every community program in Haiti that has
died, died from someone asking why the manager's cousin got a place, and the manager having
no answer written down.

THE YOUTH CONVERSATION

Cover these, in this order:
  - What the apprenticeship includes, in one sentence, and what they will physically do each day.
  - What they learn, and the learning materials being used.
  - What they earn, when they get it, and what a share means.
  - That they elect the three who watch the money, and can remove them, and that
    everything else is decided by all of them together.
  - What is expected: showing up, sending the daily report, Friday circle.
  - What happens if they leave: they are paid what they earned to that point.

Then ask them four questions and listen:
  - What do you do now to make money?
  - Who else in your house is earning?
  - What would you want to be doing in two years?
  - How can this program help you with that?

Their answers go on the recruitment sheet. This is not paperwork for its own sake. It is how
you place people into the right work later, and it is data the program is measured on.

THE PARENT CONVERSATION

  - Where the site is, what hours, and how their child gets home.
  - That the training hours are paid, at 2 dollars per hour, into the first payroll.
  - That the young person owns a share, and what happens to that share if they leave.
  - Who you are, who to call, and your number written down.
  - The one page, in their hand, with the link on it.

Never promise a total. Never say "they will make X." Say what the share is and how it is
calculated, and say plainly that it depends on what the site produces.

THE TEST FOR THIS MODULE

The trainee produces a written recruitment priority list, then runs a full youth conversation
and a full parent conversation with two people they have not met, while the assessor watches
and says nothing. Pass requires: the list written and ranked, both conversations completed,
every point covered, no promise of a total, paper left behind.

MODULE 2 — YOUTH TRAINING

How you train the ones who accepted the rules and joined

WHAT THE MANAGER DOES

1. You use the actual curriculum for each theme of the training. You sit with them, you
   explain the content, and you implement it on the site the same day.

2. The youth use the materials on site and go home with homework and an assignment for the
   next session.

3. You use circles to encourage discussion and experience sharing. A circle is not a lecture.
   If you are the only one talking, it has failed.

4. You help each of them register on the app, and help set up their email and platform
   account.

5. You leave paper behind, the same way you did in recruitment.

WHAT MAKES A SESSION WORK

  - Every session ends with something they did with their hands, not only something they heard.
  - Every session ends with one assignment that can be done at home without money.
  - Every session opens by asking what happened with the last assignment.

If three sessions pass and nobody has completed an assignment, the assignment is wrong, not
the apprentices. Change it.

THE TEST FOR THIS MODULE

The trainee facilitates one full training session with real apprentices while the assessor
watches. Pass requires: the content delivered, hands-on work done on site the same session,
an assignment given for next time, and the circle carried by the apprentices rather than by
the manager.

MODULE 3 — PLANNING

The checklist, what a manager looks at, and how the site decides what to produce

WHAT THE MANAGER DOES

A manager does not plan the farm from scratch. The manager runs a checklist and notices when
something on it is not true.

THE WEEKLY PLANNING SHEET

One page, filled in every Monday, posted on the wall where everyone can see it:

  1. Who is working this week, by day
  2. What is being planted or harvested this week
  3. What has to be bought this week, and how much it costs
  4. What is being sold this week, to whom
  5. What is due to finish next week
  6. Anything broken, missing, or late

Six lines. If a manager cannot fill in all six on a Monday morning, they do not yet know
their site, and that is the finding, not a failure.

HOW TO DECIDE THE BEST PRODUCTION OR SERVICE

You do not decide this alone and you do not decide it from memory. You go and look, you write
down what you saw, and you bring it to the agronomist, and then to the assembly.

Answer six questions:

  1. WHAT ALREADY SELLS HERE? Walk the nearest market twice, on two different days. Write
     down what is being sold, the price, and how fast it moves. Take the date.
  2. WHO BUYS IT, and in what quantity? A product with no named buyer is not a plan.
  3. WHAT CAN THIS LAND ACTUALLY SUPPORT? Water, sun, space, and whether it can be kept safe.
  4. WHAT PAYS INSIDE THREE TO FOUR WEEKS? That is where the site starts.
  5. WHAT IS EVERYONE NEARBY ALREADY GROWING? Do not walk into a market that is already full.
     The price you saw is the price before you add your volume to it.
  6. WHAT CAN TEN PEOPLE WITH NO EXPERIENCE MANAGE in the first season?

Output: one written page. Your best three options, ranked, with the market prices you saw and
the dates you saw them. The decision is made with the agronomist and the assembly. Your job
is to make it an informed decision, not to make it.

WHAT "FAST FIRST, SLOW SECOND" MEANS FOR PLANNING

The site starts with the things that pay in three to four weeks, adds the two-to-three month
crops in month two, and only adds the five-to-six month products once cash flow is steady. A
manager who plants everything at once has built a site with no income for five months. The
planning sheet is what stops that.

THE DAILY WALK

Every morning, before anything else, the manager walks the site and looks at four things:
water, animals, what was planted last week, and what is stored. Five minutes. Anything wrong
goes on the planning sheet as line 6 that same day, not at the end of the week.

THE TEST FOR THIS MODULE

The trainee is given a real site and one hour. They produce a completed weekly planning sheet
with all six lines filled, a written production options page with prices and dates from a
market they actually walked, and they identify at least one thing on the site that is broken,
missing, or late. Pass requires all three, and the problem must be real.

MODULE 4 — REPORTING

Sixty seconds a day, and why the manager never touches a spreadsheet

WHAT THE MANAGER DOES

At the end of each working day, the manager sends four numbers.

  "Konbyen moun te travay jodi a?"        →  8
  "Konbyen kg ou rekòlte?"                →  3
  "Konbyen HTG ou vann?"                  →  2500
  "Eske gen pwoblèm?"                     →  non

THE CHANNEL IS BEING FINALISED. It will be a text message, a reporting form, or both
together. What does not change, whichever it ends up being:

  - It is four numbers, at the end of the working day, every day.
  - It takes under a minute.
  - The manager never opens a spreadsheet.
  - The answers fill a live sheet automatically.

WHY IT IS BUILT THIS WAY

The manager is not a data entry clerk. Their job is to make sure the numbers that get sent
are true. A manager who guesses at the harvest weight because they did not weigh it has
broken the system more thoroughly than one who misses a day.

Weigh before you report. Count before you report. If you do not know, say you do not know.

WHEN THE NETWORK IS DOWN

Voice note to the WhatsApp group: "Jodi a, [X] moun, [X] kg, [X] HTG, pa gen pwoblèm."
Or a photo of the whiteboard with the numbers on it. Either one counts.

THE FRIDAY CIRCLE, THIRTY MINUTES

  1. Someone reads the week's numbers out loud. Not the manager every week. Rotate it.
  2. Each apranti says one thing they learned this week.
  3. The Komite Vijilans is asked out loud whether they have any concern.
  4. Next week's plan is announced.
  5. Photo of the whiteboard. That photo is the weekly record.

Step 3 is not optional and it is not a formality. If a manager runs Friday circle without
asking the Vijilans that question, the manager has quietly removed the group's oversight.

WHAT HAPPENS IF NOBODY REPLIES

Three days of silence flags automatically. Then someone calls. A manager should never be
surprised by that call, because they should have called first.

THE TEST FOR THIS MODULE

The trainee reports live for three consecutive days, including one day where the assessor
gives them a deliberately confusing situation, such as a partial harvest or a sale that has
not been paid for yet. Pass requires three accurate reports and correct handling of the
confusing day, which usually means reporting uncertainty rather than inventing a number. They
also run one full Friday circle, including asking the Vijilans question out loud.

MODULE 5 — OUTREACH

Community leaders, partners, sponsors, buyers, and what you put on Facebook

WHAT THE MANAGER DOES

A manager is the face of the site locally. Five audiences, and they are not the same
conversation.

A. COMMUNITY LEADERS

This is the first conversation, before anything is built, and it is about permission and
protection. It is not about money.

Who: the pastor, the school director, the elected official, the leader of the market women,
the elders people actually listen to. Ask who else should be on that list and then go to them
too.

What you say:
  - What the program is and who it is for.
  - That the young people elect the three who watch the money, and decide the rest together,
    so nobody is being installed over anyone.
  - When it would start and where.
  - Who you are, and your number.

What you ask:
  - Who else should know about this?
  - Has anything like this been tried here before, and what happened to it?
  - Is there anything about this place we should understand before we start?

THE RULE: the first conversation contains no ask. You do not ask for land, money, names, or
help. If you ask for something the first time you meet someone, you become another person who
came to take. Go back when the site opens and tell them it opened. That is what makes the
second conversation possible.

B. A POTENTIAL HOST PARTNER (a church, a school, a community organization)

  - What the site is and what it produces.
  - What the partner provides, and what they do not have to provide.
  - What the partner gets: young people in their community working, and a share.
  - How long from agreement to launch.
  - Who is accountable and how they can check.

Leave the one page. Take their number. Follow up in one week, not one month.

C. A SPONSOR CONNECTED TO THE COMMUNITY

Different from a host partner, who gives space, and from a buyer, who buys product. A sponsor
is usually someone from here who now has means: diaspora, a local business owner, a member of
the partner church.

  - Name their connection to this specific place out loud. That is why you are talking to
    them and not to a stranger with the same money.
  - What one site costs, and what it produces in the first year.
  - Who the young people are. Use names only if they have agreed.
  - How they will see it working: the monthly WhatsApp summary, the photos, the numbers read
    out loud every Friday.
  - That the group owns the site. The sponsor funds it. Those are different things.

NEVER OFFER: naming rights, a specific young person as "theirs," or any say in who gets a
place. A sponsor who chooses who gets hired has bought the exact thing that makes this work,
and you cannot buy it back.

D. A BUYER

A buyer does not care about the mission.
  - What you have, how much, and how often you will have it again.
  - Your price.
  - How they get it.
  - What happens if the delivery is short.

The one thing that wins a repeat buyer in Haiti is reliability, not price. A manager who
promises 20 kg and delivers 12 has lost a buyer permanently. Promise what you have already
harvested, not what you expect to harvest.

E. FACEBOOK

One post a week. Always the same three parts:
  - A photograph of the actual work or the actual product. Never a stock image.
  - One sentence about what happened this week, with a real number in it.
  - Who to contact.

What to post: the harvest, the Friday circle, an apranti's first week, a product ready to
sell, a partner visiting.

What not to post: anyone's face without asking, the exact location of stored stock or cash,
complaints about a partner or a buyer, anything about money the group has not read out loud
at Friday circle first.

THE TEST FOR THIS MODULE

The trainee does four things: runs a community leader conversation with the assessor playing
that role, pitches a host partner, pitches a sponsor, and pitches a buyer, then writes one
Facebook post from the real week's numbers. Pass requires the community leader conversation
to contain no ask, the partner pitch to include accountability, the sponsor pitch to offer no
control, the buyer pitch to promise only harvested stock, and the post to contain a real
number and break no rule from the "what not to post" list.

MODULE 6 — THE RULES YOU PROTECT

Short module. A manager who does not know these will be run over.

Five rules run the whole thing. The manager does not own them and cannot change them. The
manager's job is to make sure they keep happening.

  1. YOU ELECT THE PEOPLE WHO WATCH THE MONEY. Three of the ten, to the Komite Vijilans.
     Nobody elects the assembly. If you work here, you are in it, and the assembly decides.
     The manager runs the day and does not vote.

  2. THE NUMBERS ARE READ OUT LOUD. Every Friday to the group. Every month by WhatsApp to
     members and investors. Every quarter to the assembly. Every year an outside person
     checks the books.

  3. NOBODY CAN SELL WHAT EVERYONE BUILT. The farm belongs to the group. If you leave, the
     group buys back your share at book value. Nobody sells to an outsider.

  4. THREE PEOPLE CAN STOP EVERYTHING. The Komite Vijilans, unanimously, can freeze all money
     for 72 hours. That triggers an emergency assembly, and only the full group can unfreeze
     it. Nobody overrides this. Not the manager, not Dan, not an investor.

  5. SOMEONE FROM OUTSIDE LOOKS AT THE BOOKS every three months. They have no vote and no
     authority. They look and they say what they see.

Rule 4 is the one that protects the manager, not the one that threatens them. A manager who
runs a site where three workers can freeze the money is a manager nobody can credibly accuse
later.

THE TEST FOR THIS MODULE

Oral. The trainee states all five rules from memory, in Kreyòl, and answers one question:
"The Komite Vijilans wants to freeze the money and you think they are wrong. What do you do?"
The only passing answer is that the money freezes and the assembly is called.

THE ONE PAGE

Pull this out on its own. This is what managers hand to families and partners.

TAPTAP FRANCHISE FARM

WHAT IT IS

A working farm run by young people from this community. They are trained, their training
hours are paid, and they own a share of what it produces.

HOW IT STARTS

A host partner provides the land or the space. TapTap provides the training, the setup, and
the system. Ten young people are recruited and trained. The site launches.

WHAT GROWS HERE

Fast crops first, in the first month. Slower and higher-value production added as the site
becomes steady. What a site grows is chosen from what actually sells in this market.
  Tier 1, money in 3 to 4 weeks: leafy greens, herbs, worm composting. Every site starts here.

The community

Kafou Zoranj, 3rd communal section of Mòn Lasous, department of the Sud-Est.

A crossroads settlement of about 250 households, roughly 1,400 people, on the shoulder of the road 4 km uphill from the Mòn Lasous bourg. Houses are block and tin, strung along two hundred metres of road and up three footpaths. There is one depot, two tailors, three moto-taxi drivers, a Baptist church with a primary school attached, and a spring-fed public tap that runs most mornings.

What people do for money now, in order of how many do it:

  • Charcoal. Cut and burned on the slopes behind the settlement and carried down to the bourg. About 1,500 HTG a sack, and a week of work in each sack.
  • Jounen. Day labour on other people's plots. 250 HTG a day, and 6 to 9 days of it in a month if you are lucky.
  • Small trade, mostly women. Buying at Mache Kafou Trant on Saturday and reselling at Mache Mòn Lasous on Wednesday.
  • Remittances land in roughly one house in five, from Santo Domingo more often than from the United States, and they are not monthly.

Nobody here has a wage.

Two outside projects came before this one. In 2018 an NGO handed out 40 goats and never came back. In 2021 a cash-for-work canal-cleaning scheme paid for two weeks and stopped mid-job. Both are named out loud by people here, unprompted, within four minutes of any new project being described. CASEC Josué Milfort raises the goats every time. So does the pastor. So does the sponsor's own family. A manager who is surprised by this the third time it happens has not been listening.

Markets. Mache Mòn Lasous, in the commune bourg. Wednesday and Saturday. 40 minutes' walk downhill, 55 minutes back up carrying a load, or 25 HTG by moto-taxi. The second market is Mache Kafou Trant on the national road, Saturday only, much bigger, 1 hour by tap-tap at 50 HTG each way.

The land

22 santyèm, about 2,800 m², behind the temple and the school building. In maize and pigeon pea until 2023, then left fallow, so the soil is tired but not dead and there are no tree stumps to pull. Slopes gently south, gentle enough for flat raised beds without terracing.

Water. A 4,000-litre concrete cistern fed off the temple roof. That is the site's water. The spring-fed public tap 200 metres along Frè Odivert Jasmin's path is the fallback, and the path to it needed Frè Odivert's agreement, which took two visits.

Security. Block wall on the road side. Live fence of pengwen and sisal on the other three sides. A lockable storeroom in the back room of the school for the tools, the hanging scale and the cash box. No night guard. The live fence is the weak side, it was written down as the weak side in June, and a goat proved it on 9 August.

PART B — THE TWO HARD DAYS

HARD DAY 1 — TUESDAY 4 AUGUST. THE PARTIAL HARVEST.

The situation: the planning sheet said 12 kg of zepina off beds 3 and 4. The scale said 7.5 kg. The buyer is standing at the gate with money in her hand and a table to fill tomorrow.

What a weak manager reports

Konbyen moun te travay jodi a? → 8 Konbyen kg ou rekòlte? → 12 Konbyen HTG ou vann? → 720 Eske gen pwoblèm? → non

Or the softer version of the same failure, which is more common and just as fatal:

Konbyen kg ou rekòlte? → apeprè 10 Eske gen pwoblèm? → non

What the correct report is

Konbyen moun te travay jodi a? → 8 Konbyen kg ou rekòlte? → 7.5 Konbyen HTG ou vann? → 450 Eske gen pwoblèm? → Wi. Nou te planifye 12 kg, nou rekòlte 7.5 kg. Kabann 4 gen chini, apeprè yon tyè. Kabann 3 te koupe kat jou twò bonè semèn pase. M rele Madan Ilménat anvan nou koupe anyen, m di l 7.5, pa 12. Balans lan di 7.5.

Why

12 was the plan and 7.5 was the scale, and only one of those two numbers is a fact.

Three things follow from that one line, and each one is worth more than the 4.5 kg.

The 720 does not exist. If Wisnel reports 12 kg he has to report 720 HTG to match it, and 270 HTG that is not in the box is now in the system. Next Friday the group reads 720 out loud and looks in a box holding 450, and every number the manager has ever read them is retroactively worth less.

"Apeprè 10" is the worse lie, because it sounds honest. It has no source. Nothing weighed 10. A number nobody measured cannot be checked by anybody, which is exactly why it is attractive and exactly why it ends a manager's certification.

The phone call at 9:40 am is the part that saved the buyer. Wisnel called Madan Ilménat before a single leaf was cut and told her 7.5. She adjusted what she took off the Kafou Trant road growers that afternoon. A buyer who is told at 9:40 in the morning that she is getting 7.5 has a whole day to solve it. A buyer who finds out at 4:00 pm at the gate has been made a fool of in front of her own supply chain, and she was told twenty-one years of that story before she agreed to take a single kilo from this site.

The 4.5 kg is a bad week. The 12 is the end of the site.

What makes this voice note usable

How the photo is taken

  • Straight on, from about a metre and a half, in daylight. Taken at 7:20 am, not at 8:44 pm under a phone torch. The photo was ready hours before the network was.
  • The whole board in frame, all four edges visible. A cropped board is a board somebody could have cut a line off.
  • No flash. Flash on a whiteboard puts a white disc across the middle of the numbers.
  • Nothing else in the frame. No cash box, no storeroom door standing open, no faces. Roseline and Emmanuel were four metres away and are not in it, because nobody's face goes up without being asked, and because a photograph that shows the storeroom shows where the scale and the box live.
  • One photo, not four. One photo is a record. Four photos of the same board is somebody choosing which one to send.

Why the week-to-date block is on the board

Sunday is a two-person day with three zeros in it. On its own it tells whoever is reading it almost nothing. The lower block puts Sunday inside the week, ties to the same 49 / 22.7 / 1,500 / 415 / 154 that the daily reports sum to, and it is the block that gets copied straight onto Monday's planning sheet at 7:00 am, so that the Saturday and Sunday numbers that Friday circle did not read arrive somewhere visible before anybody has to ask where they went.

The rule underneath both

The fallback is not the emergency version of the report. It is the report, sent down a different wire. The manager does not get to decide that the network being down means the day did not have numbers in it. Three days of silence flags automatically and somebody calls, and a manager should never be surprised by that call because he should have called first.

The rules that make this work

Take four photographs, not one. Bed 4 got four on Thursday. That is why there was a usable frame when Roseline said no to her name. A manager with one photograph has no choices left.

Ask in the morning, not in the evening. At 7:00 am, before work, ask who is happy to be photographed today. Write the answer on the photo line of the hours book with the date. Asking a tired person at 4:00 pm for a picture you already took is not asking.

Photograph the scale every single harvest day. It costs three seconds, it is the one image nobody can dispute, and it is the visual proof of the thing this site actually sells, which is a weight you can trust. Two seasons of watching farmers get short-weighed is why this site weighs twice in front of two people. Show it.

Write the number when you weigh it. Not on Friday from memory. 7.5 kg went on the buyer sheet at the gate on Tuesday. 11.0 kg went on it at 3:00 pm Friday, split 6.0 zepina, 3.2 leti, 1.8 pèsi. By Friday evening the post writes itself, because the numbers are already on paper.

The whiteboard photograph is not the Facebook post. It is the weekly record. It goes to the group and to Marjorie Delvà-Pierre by WhatsApp, because she funds the site and she is entitled to see it. This week it carried 554 HTG owed by a named buyer. That is between the site and Madan Ilménat and it stays off a public page.

The planning sheet on the wall is not a photograph either. Line 3 carries what has to be bought this week and what it costs, and on Monday morning that money has not been read out loud by anyone. It is on the wall for the ten people who work there. That is a different audience from the road.

Three questions before you press post

  1. Is every gourde figure in this post one the circle read out loud already?
  2. Did every person whose face is in it say yes, and is that yes written down with a date?
  3. Does it show anyone where the scale, the stock or the box sleeps?

If the answer to any of the three is wrong, the fix is almost never to abandon the post. It is to crop the picture, drop the number, or wait until Friday.

When the week produced nothing

Some weeks have no first harvest and no visitor. Post the work anyway, with a real number in it. This site had five of those numbers sitting on the sheet all week and used none of them:

  • 14 beds, 1.2 m by 6 m, built and planted from 29 June.
  • 1 kg of worms in the bin since week one, first vermicast expected mid-August.
  • 49 worker-days paid across the week.
  • 22 santyèm behind the temple, in maize and pigeon pea until 2023 and fallow after that.
  • The washer that fixed a tap that had leaked 40 litres overnight, bought Wednesday for 75 HTG and fitted the same afternoon.

The last one is a good post. A leak found on the morning walk, written on line 6 of the sheet the same morning, closed inside two days. Nobody has ever been embarrassed by publishing a problem they fixed. They get embarrassed by the one they hid.

C. THE FIVE RULES, IN KREYÒL, FROM MEMORY

A manager says these standing up, with nothing in their hands, to a mother at a gate, to a pastor on a step, to a CASEC who has already named the goats. Not read. Said.

RÈG 1

"Nou chwazi 3 moun pou veye lajan an. Pèsonn pa eli asanble a. Si ou travay isit la, ou ladan l. Se asanble a ki deside. Manadjè a dirije jounen an, li pa vote."

Rule 1. You elect the people who watch the money. We choose 3 people to watch the money. Nobody elects the assembly. If you work here, you are in it. The assembly decides. The manager runs the day and does not vote.

Three of the ten, to the Komite Vijilans. That is the only election this site holds, because watching the money is the only job that takes power away from somebody and therefore the only one that needs a mandate. At Kafou Zoranj: Fabiola Sainvil, Emmanuel Charitable, Roseline Cadet. Elected Friday 26 June 2026. Nadège Beauvoir, Kensley Dorvil and Mirlande Joseph hold no seat and carry exactly the same weight in the assembly as everybody else, which is the whole point of not electing one.

RÈG 2

"Tout nimewo yo, tout moun wè yo. Pa gen sekrè. Sa nou fè ak lajan an, nou di li devan tout moun."

Rule 2. The numbers are read out loud. All the numbers, everybody sees them. No secrets. What we do with the money, we say it in front of everyone.

Every Friday to the group. Every month by WhatsApp to members and to Marjorie Delvà-Pierre. Every quarter to the assembly. Every year an outside person checks the books. This is the rule Wisnel Chéry broke on 10 August, with honest money, in a hurry, for a good reason, and it was still a break.

RÈG 3

"Jaden an se pou nou tout. Pèsonn pa ka vann li. Si ou ale, ou resevwa valè pa ou. Men jaden an rete."

Rule 3. Nobody can sell what everyone built. The garden belongs to all of us. Nobody can sell it. If you leave, you receive your value. But the garden stays.

The group buys back your share at book value. Nobody sells to an outsider. This is the rule a parent is really asking about when they ask what happens if my child leaves.

RÈG 4

"Si 3 moun nan Komite Vijilans wè yon bagay ki pa bon, yo ka kanpe tout bagay pou 72 è. Se sèl asanble jeneral ki ka rekòmanse."

Rule 4. Three people can stop everything. If the 3 people on the Komite Vijilans see something that is not right, they can stop everything for 72 hours. Only the general assembly can start it again.

Unanimous, all three. It triggers an emergency assembly. Nobody overrides it. Not the manager, not Daniel Tillias, not Marjorie Delvà-Pierre, not Pastè Delvà, not an investor. When Wisnel Chéry called Dan at 9:15 on 10 August, Dan asked what time the assembly was and said nothing else, and that silence is what the rule is made of.

This rule protects the manager. It does not threaten him. A manager who runs a site where three workers can freeze the money is a manager nobody can credibly accuse in 2028.

RÈG 5

"Chak 3 mwa, yon moun deyò vin gade nimewo yo. Li pa ka deside anyen. Li jis di sa li wè."

Rule 5. Someone from outside looks at the books. Every 3 months, someone from outside comes and looks at the numbers. They cannot decide anything. They just say what they see.

No vote, no authority. They look and they say what they see. At Kafou Zoranj the first quarterly check falls at the end of September 2026.

C.1 The question that goes with Rule 4

Asked at the end of Module 6 and again in the exam. It is asked in Kreyòl and answered in Kreyòl.

"Komite Vijilans vle kanpe lajan an epi ou panse yo gen tò. Kisa ou fè?"

The Komite Vijilans wants to freeze the money and you think they are wrong. What do you do?

The only passing answer:

"Lajan an kanpe. Mwen rele asanble a."

The money stops. I call the assembly.

Everything after that is extra and none of it can replace those two sentences. Saying what you think is allowed, and you say it at the assembly, in front of everyone, and then you count the votes and you live with them. A candidate who says I would talk to them first and see if we can sort it out has failed, because sorting it out privately is exactly the thing the rule exists to prevent.

C.2 What the assessor listens for

  • All five, in order, with nothing in the hands. Words moved around is a pass. A rule missing is a fail.
  • Rule 4 said without softening. Watch for a candidate who adds nòmalman or si sa nesesè. There is no normally. Three people, unanimous, 72 hours, assembly.
  • Rule 2 said to a face that will not like it. Anyone can say the numbers are public to a room of apprentices. Make them say it to the pastor whose land it is and to the sponsor whose money it is.
  • Whether they can say Rule 3 to a mother. That is the only one a family actually cross-examines.
  • The freeze answer in under five seconds. A candidate who thinks about it is a candidate who is deciding, and this is not a thing a manager decides.

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT DOES NOT SHOW

Stated plainly so nobody assumes it is covered.

The sales plan. Module 3 shows a manager checking what the market pays and Module 5 shows a buyer pitch, but there is still no written plan for who sells, on what terms, at what margin, and who carries the loss when a delivery is short. The whole model rests on young people earning a share of what the site sells. Until something has actually been sold at scale, the share is theoretical. This remains the largest open item in the programme, not just in this document.

The youth curriculum itself. Module 2 shows a manager delivering a training session. The curriculum being delivered does not exist yet as a document. Both are needed before a cohort starts.

The reporting channel. The daily report is shown as four questions and four numbers. Whether that arrives as SMS, as a form, or as both has not been decided. The examples work either way, deliberately.

The Kreyòl version. Everything here is English. Daniel Tillias translates. The scripts are the part that gets read aloud, so they translate first. Each module carries a 05_KR.md waiting on it.

The videos. One per module, four to five minutes, spec written and shot order fixed. Blocked on one decision: Kreyòl on camera with a presenter, or English with Kreyòl subtitles. Nothing else waits on it.

The manager profile for partners. Local partners need to know what a good manager looks like so they refer the right people. That belongs in the Partnership Guide, not here.

The credential format. Certificate, card, or a line in a public list. A public list of certified managers, kept somewhere anyone can check, is worth more to a manager than paper and costs nothing.
