[!success] Satisfied The Catholic parish network provides a pre-existing trust infrastructure. Subscribers do not have to trust a new brand. They have to trust their priest. That trust has been built over decades of baptisms, funerals, weddings, and Sunday meals.

Dan’s March 31, 2026 voice note, transcribed verbatim, captured what HSSNE has been saying for years:

[!quote] Daniel Tillias, Fatraka co-founder, March 31 2026 “They have been looking. For years. They tell me every time we sit down, Dan, we want to do something, we want to send something back, but every time we give, it goes somewhere and nothing comes back. Not even a photograph. They are looking for the real recipe. Something that works, something they can see, something that does not disappear into the ground.”

HSSNE’s prior deployments followed what the vault calls the Gabrielle model: trust a credentialed agronomist, fund the land, hope for accountability through personal reputation. This model fails for reasons that are not personal. They are structural:

  1. No denominator. Without a membership base and a defined unit of production, you cannot measure extraction, leakage, or surplus. Every dollar goes into a single accounting bucket and comes out as “crops were planted.”
  2. No gatekeeper. The funder and the operator are the only two parties. If the operator underdelivers, the funder either absorbs the loss silently or severs the relationship permanently. There is no middle layer that can red-flag a milestone without blowing up the partnership.
  3. No cascade. A Gabrielle deployment produces Gabrielle’s outputs. It does not produce a replicable unit that spawns another unit. The $1 deployed yesterday and the $1 deployed tomorrow are independent events.
  4. No narrative ownership. HSSNE writes a check. The agronomist does the work. There is nothing for the priest to say from the pulpit on Sunday other than “we gave.” Flock engagement dies the week after the homily.

2.2 What Is Different About Fatraka × BARSS

THE 10 QUESTIONS

Q1: “Is this just another NGO that will take the money?”

Answer: No. This is not an NGO. It is a cooperative business owned by the Haitian youth who work in it. NGOs take donations and pay salaries to administrators. Fatraka × BARSS sells a product (the Harvest Box) and the youth are co-owners of the units that produce it. Ninety percent of every dollar either goes to Haiti or builds the coop’s own capital. Nothing is donated. Nothing is charity. You are buying a box of Haitian goods from Haitian workers.

Q2: “What makes Wesley and Dan different from people who failed us before?”

Answer: Dan has done this work for 20 years in Cité Soleil through SAKALA. His garden never closed, even when kidnappings were at their worst. Parishioners who visit Haiti can see it. Wesley’s research on extraction has been validated by academics at Duke, UConn, Princeton, and the Haiti Ambassador to France. They are not outsiders. Dan is Haitian, lives in Haiti, and has receipts. Wesley is a Haitian-American whose work is in the public record. Both are available by phone, email, or site visit.

Q3: “How do I know the money actually reaches Haiti?”

Answer: Every quarter, every subscriber receives an impact report with photos of the youth, wage receipts, unit production numbers, and a line-by-line breakdown of where their $90 went. The gold reserve is published publicly with an independent audit signed by Jethro Joseph. You can request to visit a unit in person… Dan will arrange it. This is what we call the real recipe. Transparency is not a promise. It is the delivery schedule.

Q4: “What if I can’t afford $360 a year?”

Answer: There is a smaller plan called the Pratik Box … $45 per quarter, $180 per year. It ships four times a year with basic Haitian products. You can also subscribe as a group with family members or fellow parishioners. Parish groups can also invest institutionally, where the whole parish sponsors one youth or one unit. No one is excluded by price. Speak to the parish hall volunteer after Mass and we will find the plan that fits.

Q5: “Is the Harvest Box quarterly shipment really coming?”

Answer: Yes. The Year 1 production schedule is already set and the shipping partner is contracted. The first shipment window opens 90 days after you subscribe. If a shipment is delayed due to weather, ports, or political conditions in Haiti, you will receive an honest update from Dan directly, not a corporate email. We will never ghost a subscriber. If a box cannot ship, your money either rolls forward or is refunded… your choice.

Q6: “Why gold reserves? Isn’t that holding back youth wages?”

Answer: The gold reserve is the youth’s protection against the Haitian gourde losing value. When the gourde devalues (and it has many times), wages paid today are worthless next year. Gold holds its value. Ten cents of every dollar goes into this reserve so the youth keep buying power over the long term. This is not money taken from wages. It is money that protects wages. Jethro Joseph audits and publishes the reserve every quarter. Any youth worker can request to see the holdings.

Q7: “How does this compare to the Gabrielle-agronomist model that didn’t work?”

Answer: Good question. That model paid foreign agronomists to train Haitian farmers on someone else’s land with someone else’s capital, and when the funding ended the project collapsed. Fatraka × BARSS flips every part of that. The youth are on their own land (or long-term coop-controlled land), they are the owners not the trainees, there is no foreign capital to withdraw, and the revenue comes from diaspora subscriptions (us) not foundation grants. If HSSNE subscriptions drop, the coop scales down but does not die. The war chest sustains it. The model is built to survive the collapse of any single funder… including us.

Q8: “What happens if the US immigration situation changes?”

Answer: Nothing happens to the coop. The youth are in Haiti, not the US. The cooperative operates under Haitian law and does not depend on US immigration status for any worker. On the subscriber side, the Harvest Box ships to any US address (citizen, green card, TPS, or otherwise). We do not collect immigration data and we never share subscriber information with government agencies. If TPS ends and families return to Haiti, they can subscribe from there or receive boxes from family in the US. The model is TPS-proof by design.

Q9: “Can my parish organization invest as a group, not just individuals?”

Answer: Yes. This is called the Track 1 Institutional path. A parish, a Knights of Columbus chapter, a women’s sodality, or any parish organization can subscribe as a group. The group pays a bundled rate (ask for current pricing, usually $75/quarter per group member with minimum 10), receives a shared Harvest Box plus individual allocations, and gets a dedicated HSSNE hub update in the quarterly report. Some parishes sponsor a named youth or a named unit. Dan and Wesley can meet with your parish council to design the right fit.

Q10: “Where do I send someone who wants to know more?”

Answer: Three paths. In person: after any Mass, the parish hall … Dan or a trained volunteer is there on announcement Sundays. Online: the website fatraka-barss.org/hssne has the full prospectus, videos of Dan on the ground, and the subscription form. If someone has a complex concern… legal, financial, theological… send them to me and I will connect them to the right person. Nobody gets lost in this process.

[!abstract] Summary This is a formal invitation to HSSNE leadership to visit the [[Fatraka × BARSS]] ground operation in Pignon, Haiti, for three days during Q3 2026. Purpose

The purpose of this visit is simple. HSSNE leadership has asked, for years, for the real recipe. We have now asked you to trust a partnership with us. It would be wrong of us to ask for that trust without giving you the chance to see the operation with your own eyes.

Three days is enough to see:

  • The ground reality of Pignon (the town, the Nord department, the context)
  • The operational infrastructure (Treasury, Hub check-in protocol, Subscriber portal, quarterly Dashboard)
  • The first youth cohort actually working
  • The financial transparency stack (quarterly reports, gold reserve audit, member impact receipt samples)
  • The Fatraka ground team who would be accountable to HSSNE’s named Hub

Three days is not enough to play tourist. This visit is working. We are asking for your time, not your vacation.

Q3 2026. Preferred window: mid-August through late September 2026.

  • Year 1 HSSNE Hub will be operational (target go-live: July 2026)

  • First quarterly financial report will be published
  • First gold reserve audit will have been completed

Alternate windows if Q3 is not workable for HSSNE leadership calendars:

  1. All ground transport is pre-planned, vetted, and escorted
  2. No travel through Port-au-Prince is required or recommended for this itinerary
  3. Fatraka ground team coordinates with Pignon civic authorities on timing and routes
  4. Emergency contact tree (diocesan, Fatraka, US Embassy Port-au-Prince, Canadian Embassy Cap-Haitien) is shared with HSSNE pre-trip
  5. Cell coverage in Pignon is reliable. Satellite backup (Garmin inReach) is carried by the lead escort

  • Medical: Pignon has basic clinic capacity; serious cases transfer to Cap-Haitien (Justinien University Hospital or private clinic). HSSNE delegation should carry basic medical kit
