Section I

The Crisis

Haiti's orphanage system is not a care system. It is an extraction system that separates children from families, channels international donations to unaccountable operators, and in its worst manifestations feeds trafficking pipelines disguised as charitable work.

These numbers describe a system where international charity has become the economic engine driving child separation. Orphanage directors pay intermediaries, known as courtiers, to recruit children from rural families under the promise of education and a better life. Once inside the institution, children serve as fundraising instruments, their vulnerability presented to donors as proof of need. The money flows in. The accountability does not flow back.

The Orphanage-to-Outcome Pipeline

At the most extreme end, the orphanage system feeds directly into trafficking networks. Children are funneled into unregulated international adoption pipelines, trafficked across the Dominican border (an estimated 2,000 children annually), or absorbed into the restavek system of unpaid domestic child labor. Reports from field researchers and Haitian authorities have raised the possibility of organ trafficking as well, though systematized evidence remains scarce.

Australia recognized this in its 2018 Modern Slavery Act: orphanage trafficking is a form of modern slavery. The Oxford Human Rights Hub has described Haiti's orphanage system as "a form of child trafficking." Yet the data infrastructure required to trace the pipeline from intake to outcome does not exist.

Missionaries and faith-based organizations are deeply embedded in the orphanage economy. Many operate with sincere intentions. But the structural incentives of the system reward child acquisition over family preservation. And the political protection afforded to religious organizations makes reform exceptionally difficult.

The Compounding Crisis

Haiti's security collapse has made the situation more urgent. UNICEF warned in 2024 that children represent 30-50% of members in some armed groups. Over two million people have been displaced by gang violence. As the Multinational Security Support mission ramps up operations, humanitarian actors will encounter increasing numbers of children requiring protection.

Without intervention, the pattern from 2010 will repeat: temporary shelters will become permanent orphanages, donor funding will flow to institutions rather than families, and a new generation of children will be separated from parents who are alive and willing to care for them.

Community-based care costs as little as one-sixth of institutional care. The question is not whether alternatives exist. The question is whether the evidence base exists to justify the policy shift. That is what this project builds.

Section II

The Gap

The child protection field knows orphanages in Haiti are harmful. What it lacks is the systematized evidence to trace the full pipeline from intake to outcome, and the forensic data products to compel policy action.

What Exists

Significant qualitative and advocacy work has been done. Lumos has documented the scale of the problem and supported IBESR in closing 13 institutions. Overture International has facilitated family reunification for children in southern Haiti since 2020. UNICEF has provided technical guidance. Academic research, including Dr. Archer's dissertation, has illuminated the emotional and developmental harm of institutional care.

What Does Not Exist

Gap Why It Matters
Systematized orphanage-to-outcome database Case-level data linking specific orphanages to specific trafficking, adoption, or restavek outcomes has never been aggregated. Data exists in fragments across organizations. It has never been integrated.
Funding flow forensics $100M+ flows annually to Haitian orphanages. Where does it actually go? No systematic tracing of donor-to-orphanage-to-outcome financial flows exists. Donor-side accountability is nearly absent.
Missionary-orphanage network map Religious organizations operate the vast majority of Haiti's orphanages. No comprehensive map links specific missionary networks to specific institutions to specific outcomes.
Keyword-extracted pattern analysis Reunification case files contain rich qualitative data, but systematic keyword extraction and pattern recognition across cases has not been performed at scale.
Per-orphanage or per-region fact sheets IBESR needs actionable evidence documents, not academic papers. No one is producing facility-level or region-level fact sheets designed for regulatory enforcement.
Quantified extraction model The orphanage system extracts value from three populations simultaneously: children (labor, identity), families (separation), and donors (money). No framework models this as an extraction economy.

The field has the diagnosis. What it lacks is the data architecture to turn fragmented evidence into the kind of actionable intelligence that changes policy. That is the specific capability this collaboration provides.

Section IV

Methodology

A three-phase research design that transforms fragmented field evidence into systematized data products, each phase building on the last, each producing outputs that can stand alone for policy use.

Phase 1
Ethnographic Evidence
Field data collection, case file organization, qualitative coding
Phase 2
Data Architecture
Keyword extraction, pattern recognition, network mapping, financial tracing
Phase 3
Policy Products
Fact sheets, dashboards, IBESR briefs, donor reports

Phase 1: Ethnographic Evidence Collection

Lead: Dr. Archer · Months 1-4

Dr. Archer brings direct access to data that exists nowhere else in aggregated form. Overture International's reunification case files from southern Haiti (2020-present) document the journey of children from orphanage intake through family tracing to reunification outcomes. These files contain interviews, intake records, and case notes that have never been systematically analyzed.

Phase 1 involves organizing this case-level data, conducting additional ethnographic interviews where gaps exist, and establishing the qualitative coding framework that will structure the database. Dr. Archer's existing IBESR relationship, reinforced by her March 2026 re-accreditation engagement, provides the institutional access required for government data and policy input.

Phase 2: Data Architecture and Analysis

Lead: Wesley Bertil · Months 3-8

Wesley builds the analytical infrastructure to transform Phase 1's qualitative evidence into structured, queryable data. This includes:

Component Method Output
Keyword extraction NLP-assisted extraction across all reunification case files to identify recurring patterns, actors, locations, and mechanisms Pattern taxonomy and frequency analysis
Network mapping Cross-referencing orphanage operators, missionary organizations, adoption agencies, and known trafficking routes Missionary-orphanage-outcome network graph
Funding flow tracing Tracking international donor money from source through orphanage to actual expenditure, using public filings, IRS Form 990s, and field-verified spending data Per-orphanage and per-donor financial flow maps
Geospatial analysis Mapping orphanage locations against child origin locations, trafficking route data, and border crossing points Geographic risk maps for IBESR enforcement targeting
Extraction modeling Applying quantitative extraction framework to orphanage economics: who extracts what from whom, at what rate Extraction flow diagrams and value quantification

Phase 3: Policy Product Generation

Joint: Dr. Archer + Wesley Bertil · Months 6-12

The final phase produces the evidence products that IBESR, international organizations, and donor agencies need. Every product is designed for a specific audience and a specific decision. The methodology draws on BARSS Research's proven capability: the same fact-sheet generation pipeline that produced 296 TPS forensic fact sheets is adapted here for orphanage-level and region-level analysis.

Design principle: Every research output must answer a specific policy question for a specific decision-maker. Academic publication is a secondary objective. The primary objective is evidence that changes how children are protected.

Ethical Framework

This research involves vulnerable children and sensitive case data. All work adheres to:

Standard Application
Informed consent All ethnographic data collected under IRB-equivalent ethical protocols. Case files anonymized before data architecture processing.
Do no harm No publication of data that could identify specific children or endanger families. Facility-level analysis uses coded identifiers until IBESR enforcement action is initiated.
Data sovereignty Haitian institutional partners (IBESR) retain ownership of all data products. Research outputs serve Haitian policy priorities first.
Child safeguarding All team members adhere to international child safeguarding standards. No direct interaction with children for research purposes without IBESR oversight.
Section V

Proposed Deliverables

Six core data products, each designed for a specific audience and a specific policy use. Built incrementally across the project timeline so early outputs inform later analysis.

01
Orphanage-to-Outcome Database
Systematized from Overture International case files (2020-present). Links specific institutions to specific child outcomes: reunification, adoption, trafficking indicators, restavek placement, or continued institutionalization. The first integrated dataset of its kind for Haiti.
02
Keyword Extraction Analysis
NLP-assisted pattern recognition across reunification case files. Identifies recurring actors, mechanisms, geographic patterns, and risk indicators. Produces a searchable taxonomy of trafficking-adjacent language and behavior documented in case records.
03
Funding Flow Forensics
Traces where international orphanage donations actually go. Maps donor-to-orphanage-to-expenditure flows using IRS Form 990 data, field-verified spending records, and public financial disclosures. Identifies the gap between stated charitable purpose and actual fund utilization.
04
IBESR Policy Brief Series
Actionable evidence documents designed for regulatory enforcement. Each brief addresses a specific policy question: which facilities to prioritize for closure, where to deploy limited inspection resources, what legislative reforms would have the greatest impact.
05
Forensic Fact Sheets
Per-orphanage or per-region evidence profiles modeled on the Brattle Group format. Each fact sheet consolidates all available data on a facility or region: licensing status, funding sources, child outcomes, operator network, and risk indicators. Designed for institutional use.
06
Missionary-Orphanage Network Map
Comprehensive mapping of which religious and missionary organizations operate which orphanages, connected to which adoption agencies, linked to which child outcomes. Visual network graph with queryable underlying database. The first systematic map of this network for Haiti.
