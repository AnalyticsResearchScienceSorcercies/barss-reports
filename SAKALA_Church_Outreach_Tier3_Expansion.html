1. Executive Summary

Tier 3 is the expansion layer of the SAKALA x BARSS Harvest Box (Bwat Rekolt) distribution network. It activates after Florida (Tier 1, 199 churches, home delivery) and Massachusetts (Tier 2, 39 churches, coop pickup) have proven the model across two consecutive quarters of on-time fulfillment.

The plan deploys into three states... New Jersey, New York, and Georgia... leveraging Marc Guirand's confirmed relationships with 80 churches and HSNNE's community infrastructure in the NJ corridor. Each state receives a delivery model tailored to its geography: hybrid in NJ, coop pickup in Brooklyn, home delivery in metro Atlanta.

Combined with Tiers 1 and 2, the full church network reaches 318 churches, an estimated 2,234 subscribers, and roughly $802K/year in recurring cooperative revenue... all flowing through extraction-proof channels back to 25 SAKALA sites across 7 Haitian departments.

Key principle: No Tier 3 launch happens until FL + MA hit their expansion triggers. The model proves itself first. We do not scale broken supply chains.

Georgia

25 Churches · Atlanta Metro / Clarkston / Decatur / Stone Mountain

Georgia represents the fastest-growing Haitian community in the US outside the traditional Northeast/Florida corridor. Atlanta's post-2010 Haitian population boom was driven by affordable housing, job availability, and refugee resettlement infrastructure (particularly in Clarkston, sometimes called "the most diverse square mile in America").

GA Market Characteristics

  • Spread-out geography: Metro Atlanta spans 8,376 sq mi. Unlike Brooklyn, there is no walkable Haitian neighborhood. Churches are the ONLY consolidation points.
  • Car culture: 90%+ of congregants drive. This mirrors the Florida model, making home delivery the natural fit.
  • Newer community: Average Haitian residency in GA is shorter than NJ/NY. Lower average subscription uptake per church (6 vs 8-10), but high growth potential.
  • Hartsfield-Jackson: World's busiest airport. If air freight ever supplements container shipping for perishable items, Atlanta is the best-positioned Tier 3 market.

GA Strategic Note: Georgia is the proving ground for SAKALA's ability to scale beyond the traditional NE/FL Haitian corridor. Success here signals replicability in Houston, Charlotte, and other emerging diaspora cities.

3. Model Selection by State

Each state receives the delivery model that matches its population density, driving patterns, and logistics cost structure. The three models were validated in Tiers 1 and 2.

State Model Rationale Pickup / Delivery Points Cost per Box
NJ Hybrid Dense urban cores (JC, Newark) suit coop pickup. Essex County suburbs need home delivery. Split model maximizes coverage. 3-4 church hubs (pickup) + home delivery for suburban subscribers ~$5-7 blended
NY Coop Pickup Brooklyn density is unmatched. Subscribers are within walking/transit distance of their church. NYC delivery logistics cost $8-12/stop... untenable at $90/quarter. 5-6 church hubs across Brooklyn, 1 in Spring Valley, 1 in Queens ~$2-3 (venue only)
GA Home Delivery Metro Atlanta sprawl makes centralized pickup impractical. Car culture mirrors Florida. Subscribers expect doorstep delivery. Home delivery via regional carrier (OnTrac / local partner) ~$6-8

Model Definitions

Home Delivery

Box shipped directly to subscriber's address. Regional carrier handles last mile. Highest per-unit cost but lowest friction for subscriber. Proven in FL (Tier 1).

Coop Pickup

Boxes delivered in bulk to designated church. Subscribers pick up on Harvest Day (quarterly event after Sunday service). Lowest cost, highest community engagement. Proven in MA (Tier 2).

Hybrid

Dense corridors get coop pickup. Suburban/exurban subscribers get home delivery. Requires more coordination but matches mixed-density markets. New model, first deployed in NJ.

7. Partner Leverage

Tier 3 does not launch cold. Existing partnerships developed during Tiers 1 and 2 provide infrastructure, logistics, and cultural credibility that Tier 3 inherits.

HSNNE (Haiti Solidarity Network of the Northeast)

Location: Jersey City, NJ
Status: Awaiting response (as of April 2026)
Value: Community organizing infrastructure... rally lists, WhatsApp groups, event venues, existing donor base. If HSNNE activates, they become the NJ hub coordinator, managing 3-4 pickup locations and coordinating with Marc's pastoral network.

If HSNNE declines: Marc Guirand's 28 direct pastoral relationships carry the NJ launch independently. HSNNE adds efficiency, not viability. The network launches either way.

Ezrah Aharone / ILA (Center for Global Africa)

Location: Bear, DE (NJ/DE border region)
Value: ILA port relationships at Port Wilmington + potential Port Newark/Elizabeth connections. As SAKALA scales, secondary port capacity becomes essential. Aharone's APRM/AU network also positions Tier 3 within a broader Pan-African framework that resonates with church leadership.

Specific leverage: If NJ volume justifies splitting container arrivals between Port Wilmington and Port Newark, Aharone's ILA contacts facilitate the logistics negotiation.

Uncle Bobbie's Bookstore (Philadelphia)

Location: Germantown, Philadelphia
Value: Cross-market visibility. Uncle Bobbie's sits between DE (Port Wilmington) and NJ (Newark corridor). As a culturally significant Black bookstore, it amplifies the Harvest Box narrative to audiences beyond the Haitian diaspora. The almanac integration (Harvest Box includes seasonal content) gives Uncle Bobbie's a product to stock and recommend.

Specific leverage: NJ subscribers passing through Philly see SAKALA products at Uncle Bobbie's. Cultural crossover drives brand awareness without advertising spend.

Marc Guirand (Pastoral Network)

Location: Tri-state area + Atlanta
Value: Marc is the entire Tier 3 relationship layer. His confirmed relationships with 80 churches across 3 states eliminate the cold-outreach problem entirely. Every church in this plan has a warm introduction. The launch strategy is: Marc calls the pastor, explains the cooperative model, invites them to a Harvest Day event. That is the sales cycle.

Critical dependency: Marc's capacity is the binding constraint. He cannot activate 80 churches simultaneously. Hence the staggered rollout: NJ first (28), then NY + GA (52) three months later.

What $802K/Year Means for Haiti

This is not charity. This is cooperative economics. At $802K/year flowing through extraction-proof channels:

  • 25 SAKALA sites receive consistent purchase orders... roughly $32K per site per year in guaranteed demand for Haitian-grown goods.
  • 310 youth across 7 departments have a market for what they produce. No middleman. No BAM BAM family taking a cut.
  • Patronage dividends return surplus to subscribers... making membership more valuable each year.
  • Gold reserve builds through the BUILD phase (Years 5-7), converting cooperative surplus into member wealth that cannot be extracted by traditional banking mechanisms.

The math is simple: 2,234 diaspora families spending $90/quarter = $802K/year flowing directly from diaspora households to Haitian cooperative producers. No USAID. No NGO overhead. No extraction. Theta = 0. That is the point.
