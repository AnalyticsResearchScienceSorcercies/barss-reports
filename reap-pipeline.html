Standard fiscal analysis asks: "How much revenue does the State collect?" REAP asks: "How much actually reaches public purpose?" The difference is Φ — the share captured by corruption, inefficiency, and institutional leakage before it reaches a school, a road, or a clinic.

Five Key Findings

1
GDP contracts 2.2%. The tax code's burden exceeds the economy's capacity to absorb it. Revenue gains are offset by economic shrinkage. You collect more per gourde but there are fewer gourdes.
2
The poorest pay 3.6x more (relative to income) than the richest. Consumption taxes hit hardest at the bottom. The code lacks progressive mechanisms to offset this.
3
The formal economy shrinks from 30% to 23%. Higher tax burden pushes businesses into informality. The tax base contracts. The opposite of what the code intends.
4
Only 14 cents of every tax gourde reaches public purpose. Φ = 0.86. The same leakage rate as international aid. The State extracts like an NGO.
5
20 countries tested. When we applied Haiti's economic structure to the tax codes of the USA, Canada, France, and all 15 CARICOM states, every single one produced better outcomes for Haiti's population than Haiti's own code.
