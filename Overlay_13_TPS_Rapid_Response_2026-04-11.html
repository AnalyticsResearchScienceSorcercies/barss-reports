§ VI — WHAT THIS MEANS LEGALLYThe cause-of-action map.

A grant request that claims institutional authorship of an individual's work product creates specific legal exposure.

1. Copyright infringement — 17 U.S.C. § 501

The grant describes Wesley's protected expression... his selection of sectors, his arrangement of state-level data, his expert report frameworks... as institutional RFL products. Under Feist v. Rural, the selection, arrangement, and expression are Wesley's. Claiming them as RFL's is infringement.

2. CMI removal — 17 U.S.C. § 1202(b)

Wesley's vault files carry his authorship in YAML frontmatter. Presenting them to a funder as "Reparations Finance Lab has completed" with no researcher attribution strips the copyright management information. If the grant was submitted to a foundation, the CMI removal was disseminated to that funder.

3. Fraudulent grant representation

Grant applications carry implicit representations of institutional capacity. Claiming "RFL has completed a nationwide economic impact analysis" when the analysis was completed by an individual (Wesley Bertil, BARSS LLC) who is not an RFL employee misrepresents RFL's research capacity to the funder. If the funder relied on this representation in making a grant decision, the misrepresentation has financial consequences.

4. The $30,000 double-billing problem

The grant requests $30,000 for "expert witness reports for federal litigation" to be "delivered within 10 days." Those 5 reports already existed in Wesley's vault. If the grant were funded and the $30,000 disbursed, RFL would receive payment for deliverables it did not produce and that were already complete. This is the textbook fact pattern for grant fraud.

5. The "fiscal lead" problem

RFL is listed as "fiscal lead" and is a fiscally sponsored 501(c)(3). The grant funds would flow through RFL's fiscal sponsor. Wesley... the actual researcher who produced every deliverable described... is not the fiscal recipient. The grant structure ensures that the person who did the work is structurally excluded from the funding that work generates.
