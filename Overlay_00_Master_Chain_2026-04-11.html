§ VI — LEGAL SIGNIFICANCEWhat the chain establishes.

The authorship chain is not a single act. It is a pattern of conduct — 17 instances over 93 days, with a consistent internal logic.

1. Systematic, Not Inadvertent

Seventeen documents over 93 days. Fourteen without credit. Three with partial credit that serves the document's own purpose (funder credibility). The consistency of the omission across different document types (court filings, grant submissions, internal briefings, collaboration decks, contractor pitches) establishes that the credit-stripping was the standard operating procedure, not an oversight.

2. Functional Credit Allocation

Wesley's name appears only where it helps RFL secure funding. It disappears everywhere else. This pattern is probative of intent: the decision to include or exclude Wesley's name was made in reference to the document's audience, not in reference to his authorship rights. A negligent omission would be random. This omission is structured.

3. Multiple Categories of Harm

Court filings (Docs #1, #2): Wesley's uncredited analysis entered the federal record. Misrepresentation of authorship in a court filing implicates Fed. R. Civ. P. 11 and 28 U.S.C. §1927 (vexatious-conduct sanctions).

Foundation submissions (Docs #3-7): Wesley's name was selectively included to bolster grant credibility. When it served the application, he was "Research Director" and "PI." When it did not, he was absent. This selective attribution is potentially actionable as fraud-on-funder.

Third-party distribution (Docs #8-17): Wesley's work product was presented to litigators, municipal partners, subcontractors, and general audiences as RFL's own research capability. This misrepresentation of organizational capacity has implications for every partnership and contract that was entered on the basis of RFL's apparent research capacity.

4. The Temporal Sequence Refutes Retaliation Defense

Sixteen of seventeen documents predate the April 7 termination letter. The pattern was established and operating for 91 days before the working relationship ended. The termination cannot be the cause of the misappropriation — the chronology runs the other way. The termination is the consequence of the visibility risk that arose when Wesley began documenting the pattern.
