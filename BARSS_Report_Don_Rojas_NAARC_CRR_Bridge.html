Part I

The Gap

Two movements. Two continents. No shared forensic framework.

The US reparations movement and the Caribbean reparations movement are the two largest coordinated efforts to quantify and redress historical extraction from Black populations. They share origins, share perpetrators, and share the economic consequences of the same centuries-long system.

They do not share a methodology.

NAARC operates from a qualitative 10-Point Plan. CARICOM operates from a qualitative 10-Point Plan. Darity computes the US wealth gap. Craemer computes enslaved labor value. The Brattle Group produced one Caribbean damages report. UWI's Centre for Reparation Research conducts historical and qualitative analysis. Haiti's CNHRR has a single-country claim. Each of these efforts is valuable. None of them connect to each other mathematically.

Part III

Three Independent Derivations

The constant is not just observed. It is mathematically inevitable.

Any single derivation could be a coincidence. Three independent derivations from three separate fields of economics converging on the same number is a structural feature of extraction itself.

0.80 Game Theory
0.80 Public Finance
0.80–0.85 Principal-Agent Theory

Derivation 1: Tullock Contest (Game Theory)

Gordon Tullock's rent-seeking model shows that when n oligopolistic extractors compete for a fixed prize, the dissipation rate converges to (n−1)/n. At n = 5 extractors, this equals exactly 0.80. In Haiti's BAM BAM syndicate (n = 6), the predicted value is 0.83; the observed value is 0.86.

The irony: Tullock was a Virginia School architect, part of the intellectual tradition that denied systemic racism existed. His own model, applied to his own blind spot, produces the extraction constant.

Derivation 2: Gruber & Saez (Public Finance)

Emmanuel Saez (Berkeley, Clark Medal recipient) and Jonathan Gruber demonstrated in their 2002 Journal of Public Economics paper that the revenue-maximizing flat tax rate with no allowances is approximately 80%. This is the theoretical maximum that a state (or state-like entity) can extract before revenue declines due to behavioral responses.

The extraction ceiling is the same whether you call the extractor a "government" or an "elite syndicate."

Derivation 3: Subsistence Floor (Principal-Agent)

The simplest derivation: extracted populations need approximately 15–20% of produced value to survive and continue producing. This biological and economic floor constrains maximum extraction to 0.80–0.85. Below this threshold, the population collapses and extraction ceases.

This is why enslaved populations received approximately 15–20% of the value they produced as subsistence... not because enslavers were merciful, but because extraction below this level destroyed the labor force.

Why three derivations matter for NAARC and CARICOM: When defendants challenge the methodology in court, arbitration, or public debate, a constant derived from a single source can be dismissed. A constant derived from three independent fields... game theory, public finance, and principal-agent theory... requires dismantling three separate literatures simultaneously. That is the difference between a claim and a fortification.

Additional Validation: Bouchaud-Mézard Wealth Condensation

The Bouchaud-Mézard (2000) stochastic wealth condensation model derives the Dual Theta regime as a phase transition. When the ratio of speculative trading to wage income exceeds a critical threshold, wealth condenses to a small number of agents. The predicted extraction rate at condensation: 0.80. The model also predicts that Theta = 1.6× Maximum Sustainable Yield, meaning elite extraction systematically overshoots the biologically sustainable rate.
