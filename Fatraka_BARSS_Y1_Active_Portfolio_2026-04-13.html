5. The Firewall Tax Layer

The firewall costs real money. That is the honest trade. SAKALA Community Center has 23 years of institutional trust… with faith-based funders, with Cité Soleil residents, with the US Embassy, with CRS and MCC and the rest. Running the Harvest Box through SAKALA would be cheaper. It would also contaminate the trust.

So the Fatraka × BARSS structure pays a tax. Here is what the tax buys:

  • HSI fiscal sponsorship ($50K cap): Fatraka is not yet a standalone US-recognized 501c3 pass-through. HSI’s sponsorship lets subscribers donate tax-deductibly in the US and routes funds to Fatraka in Haiti. The 15% fee is standard for fiscal sponsors. Capping at $50K keeps the tax proportionate as revenue scales.
  • Duplicate audit ($15K): Fatraka needs its own books and its own audit trail. SAKALA’s auditor cannot touch Fatraka revenue without creating exactly the commingling problem the firewall exists to prevent.
  • Separate legal/compliance ($10K): MOU between BARSS and Fatraka, subscription terms of service, Stripe merchant agreement, HSI sponsorship agreement, Haiti entity registration confirmation. All of this is Fatraka-specific.
  • Pratik tech stack ($0 external): Treasury, Hub, Subscribers, Dashboard deployment is in-house at BARSS LLC on the existing deployed stack. No external build cost. No $30K Y1 line item. This keeps firewall total at $143K, not $173K.
  • Diaspora outreach ($15K): Fatraka cannot call Indriy or Middle Knight donors. Fatraka × BARSS builds its own list from BARSS diaspora research (975K Haitian diaspora mapped across 1,420 ZIPs, 1,604 churches identified). $15K is modest for this work… it is organic outreach, warm contacts, no paid ads Y1.
  • Mid-year working capital ($25K): Hub-atom architecture deploys 4 units across Y1 with a 2-3 month ramp between cash outlay and first surplus for the atoms (hubs ramp faster per Type 02 spec). This smooths the cash flow curve.
  • Misc ($28K): Travel between Port-au-Prince, Jérémie, Brooklyn, Miami, Brockton. Insurance. Contingency.

[!warning] The firewall is a feature, not a cost $143K overhead on $835K blended Y1 revenue is 17%. At Y1 Scenario B scale this is absorbed by the blended operational surplus and still leaves $53K pre-war-chest net plus a $50K Y2 war chest. By Y2, revenue rises and firewall holds roughly flat, dropping the overhead ratio to ~13%. The firewall is affordable precisely because the subscription engine runs at V4 scale from Q1.

6. Dynamic Allocation (Scenario F)

From the V4 Gold DRIP Summary, authoritative language:

[!quote] Scenario F (Dynamic Allocation) Years 1-4 (STANDARD): 10% of surplus to gold reserve. Member rewards from franchise surplus. DRIP scaling. Years 5-7 (BUILD): 65% of surplus to gold. Compounding phase. Member rewards suppressed temporarily to maximize reserve growth at peak franchise density. Years 8-10 (HARVEST): 30% gold, 45% worker wages, 25% member rewards. Reserve reaches steady state and begins distributing growth above the floor.

Why this structure

Y1-4 is the franchise deployment phase. Standard allocation (10% gold) keeps most surplus recycling into franchise buildout via DRIP. Y5-7 is the density cap phase… 344 franchises deployed, no more ground to gain in Haiti outside Cité Soleil. BUILD phase redirects the surplus into gold reserve compounding so when Y8 arrives, HARVEST has something meaningful to distribute. Y8-10 is the payoff… workers get real wages (1.8x Haiti minimum), members get real cashouts ($223/qtr), and the reserve still retains 30% of surplus to keep the floor intact.

This document commits to Scenario F as the default allocation path. Jethro has review rights at each phase transition. Standard → BUILD (Y4→Y5) and BUILD → HARVEST (Y7→Y8) are formal gate reviews.

Why Jethro should approve V4-scale gold allocation

  • Not leveraged. Physical gold, held at a US or Canadian vault custodian. No margin call scenario exists.
  • Not speculative. Fatraka is not trading gold. It is holding it as a trust anchor.
  • Funded by subscription revenue, not grants. No donor dollar buys gold. HSI-channeled grants (Sisters, future cycles) flow to operations, not reserve.

  • Balance-sheet credibility. Faith-based funders are more confident in organizations that run with discipline. A reserve is proof of discipline, not diversion.

Reserve policy

The reserve does not distribute until Year 8 (HARVEST phase). Until then it compounds. Starting Y8 under Scenario F, 25% of surplus flows to member rewards (paid quarterly), 45% to worker wage increases, and 30% continues into the reserve to keep the floor growing with inflation. The floor is always protected. Distributions come from growth above the floor.
