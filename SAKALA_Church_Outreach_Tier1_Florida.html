Section 1

Executive Summary

The Harvest Box (Bwat Rekolt) is a $90/quarter cooperative subscription box containing Haitian-grown moringa, castor oil, spices, coffee, and seasonal produce. Every box purchased funds one youth for one quarter across SAKALA's 25 operational sites in 7 Haitian departments. There are currently 310 youth in the pipeline. The box is the bridge between the diaspora's purchasing power and Haiti's agricultural labor force.

Churches are the distribution architecture. The Haitian diaspora does not organize around zip codes. It organizes around congregations. A single pastor's endorsement converts more households in one Sunday than six months of digital marketing. Marc Guirand... through his Job Power vocational training network... has existing relationships with 398 Haitian churches across the United States. 199 of them are in Florida.

Florida is Phase 1 for two reasons:

  1. Density. 975,000 Haitian diaspora members live in Florida. That is the largest concentration in the country. The 199 churches sit inside the highest-density zip codes.
  2. Home delivery. Florida is the home delivery market. Subscribers get boxes shipped to their door. No cooperative pickup infrastructure needed. Lower operational overhead. Faster launch.
The Math 199 churches. Average 60 Haitian households per congregation. 10% conversion rate. That is 1,194 subscriptions at $90/quarter = $107,460/quarter = $429,840/year in recurring cooperative revenue. Conservative. Achievable in 6 months.

The Kingdom Church in Orlando... Pastor David S. Jacques... is already a Magnificent 10 partner. TKC is the proof of concept. Every subsequent church sees what TKC did, hears what TKC's congregation experienced, and says "we want that."

Section 5

The Pastor Pitch

Marc already knows these pastors. He is not cold-calling. He is calling people who have seen him train their youth, who have hosted Job Power events, who trust him. The pitch is a conversation, not a sales script. But it hits five points every time.

Five Talking Points

1. "Your congregation already sends money to Haiti. This is a way to get something back AND send more."

The average Haitian household sends $150-300/month in remittances. That money disappears into Western Union fees and arrives as cash that gets spent on imported goods. The Harvest Box flips the flow. For $90/quarter ($30/month... less than what they spend on remittance fees alone), subscribers receive real Haitian agricultural products at their door AND fund a named youth in Haiti. The money does not disappear. It grows food, trains young people, and comes back as a box on your doorstep.

2. "Every $90 box funds one youth for one quarter. Your church can sponsor youth by name."

This is not a donation. This is a cooperative purchase with a human face. Every subscriber receives a youth sponsor card with the name, photo, site location, and training track of the SAKALA youth their subscription supports. Pastors can organize congregation-wide sponsorships... "Our church sponsors 10 youth at the Jacmel site." That becomes a point of pride. It gets announced from the pulpit. It goes in the bulletin.

3. "No cost to the church. We set up a table after service. Congregation signs up voluntarily."

Zero financial risk for the church. Zero administrative burden. SAKALA provides the table materials, the signup cards, the QR codes, and the person to staff the table. The church provides the space and the pastoral endorsement. That is the entire ask. No inventory. No handling. No accounting. The boxes ship directly to subscriber homes.

4. "The church gets 5% referral credit on every subscription."

For every subscription that originates from the church, 5% of the quarterly revenue goes into the church's Haiti mission fund. That is $4.50 per box per quarter. A church with 20 subscribers generates $360/year for its mission fund. A church with 50 subscribers generates $900/year. It is not life-changing money for the church... but it is money that arrives automatically, every quarter, with no fundraising effort. And it goes directly to the church's existing Haiti programs.

5. "Quarterly Harvest Day events at subscribing churches."

Every quarter, subscribing churches host a Harvest Day. Haitian food. Music. A live video call with SAKALA youth at a site in Haiti. Unboxing of the new seasonal box. Community celebration. SAKALA provides the event template and coordinates the Haiti video link. The church provides the space. Harvest Day turns a subscription into a community ritual. It is the thing that keeps people subscribed quarter after quarter.

Conversion Model

The model is deliberately conservative. Every assumption below is designed to undercount.

Base Assumptions

Parameter Value Rationale
Average Haitian church size 150 members Mix of mega-churches (500+) and small congregations (40-60). 150 is the weighted average.
Average households per church 60 ~2.5 members per household. Conservative... Haitian households average 3.2 persons.
Household conversion rate 10% Industry standard for church-endorsed subscription products is 12-18%. We use 10%.
Subscriptions per church 6 60 households x 10% = 6 subscriptions.
Revenue per subscription $90/quarter Anchor price point. No discounts in Year 1.
Quarterly churn 8% Subscription box industry average is 10-12%. Church social pressure reduces churn.
Organic growth (quarter-over-quarter) 5% Word-of-mouth within congregations. Net of churn = ~-3% net growth initially, then positive as Harvest Day builds community.

Section 7

Pastor Welcome Kit

Every partnering pastor receives a physical kit within 7 days of confirming participation. The kit is the toolbox. Everything needed to activate the congregation is inside. Nothing requires the pastor to create, design, or print anything.

1. Partnership Summary (1 page)
One-page overview of the program. What it is, how it works, what the church gives (space + endorsement), what the church gets (5% credit + Harvest Day events). Bilingual EN/Kreyol. Laminated. Goes on the pastor's desk or church office bulletin board.
2. Sample Harvest Box (Free)
A full-size box with actual contents: moringa powder, castor oil, spice blend, coffee, and one seasonal item. The pastor opens it. Smells it. Tastes the coffee. This is not a brochure... it is the product. First box is always free. The pastor keeps it or shares with the deacon board.
3. Congregation Flyer (50 copies)
Bilingual EN/Kreyol one-sheet for distribution to members. Front: product photos, price, youth sponsorship story. Back: QR code to sign up, list of box contents by season, 5% church credit explanation. Designed for bulletin inserts or lobby display.
4. QR Code Signup Cards (100 pack)
Business-card-sized. One side: QR code linking to church-specific signup page (tracks referrals for 5% credit). Other side: "$90/quarter. 1 box. 1 youth. Plant something." Pew-friendly. Pocket-sized. Take one, sign up at home.
5. Named Youth Sponsor Card (Sample)
Sample of what every subscriber receives: a card with the photo, name, age, SAKALA site, and training track of the specific youth their subscription funds. The pastor sees that this is not abstract charity... it is a named human being connected to each box. Each subscribing household gets their own unique youth match.
6. Harvest Day Event Guide
2-page template for hosting quarterly Harvest Day. Includes: event flow (90 minutes), suggested food/music, video call setup instructions (connect to SAKALA site), photo/social media guidelines, and attendance tracking sheet. The church runs it. SAKALA provides the Haiti connection.

The Church Whisperer

Marc Guirand is the single point of trust between SAKALA and 199 Florida churches. He built these relationships over years through Job Power's vocational training pipeline. Pastors know him. They have seen him work with their youth. They take his calls. This is not a transferable asset. If Marc does not make the call, the call does not convert.

What Marc Does NOT Do

  • Fulfillment. Marc does not pack or ship boxes. That is operations.
  • Platform management. Marc does not manage the subscription website or process payments.
  • Youth matching. Marc does not assign youth sponsors to subscribers. SAKALA operations handles this.
  • Kit printing. Marc does not produce the Welcome Kits. He delivers them.
  • Ongoing church management. Once a church is activated and running Harvest Day independently, Marc moves on. The church becomes self-sustaining.
Marc Is a Bottleneck... by Design There is no way around this. Marc's personal relationships ARE the distribution channel. The risk is obvious: if Marc is unavailable, the pipeline stalls. The mitigation is speed. Get all 199 churches activated within 6 months so the network becomes self-sustaining and no longer depends on Marc for ongoing maintenance. His job is to light the fire at each church. The congregation keeps it burning.

Appendix

Decision Framework

When a Pastor Says "Not Now"

Not every pastor will say yes on the first call. The objections are predictable and the responses are simple.

Objection Response
"We already have a Haiti mission partnership." This does not replace your existing partnership. It adds to it. The 5% referral credit goes directly into your Haiti mission fund. Your existing relationship gets more money, not less.
"My congregation can't afford $90/quarter." $90/quarter is $30/month. Less than most households spend on Western Union fees alone. And they get a box of products in return. This is not a donation... it is a purchase.
"I need to talk to my deacon board." Perfect. I will send the sample box and the one-pager. Let the deacons open the box. The products speak for themselves. Can I follow up in two weeks?
"How do I know the money gets to Haiti?" Every subscriber gets a named youth sponsor card. You can video call the SAKALA site during Harvest Day and see the youth your congregation is funding. The allocation breakdown is printed on every flyer.
"We don't allow vendors after service." This is not a vendor table. It is a church mission initiative. We set it up however you want... lobby, fellowship hall, parking lot, online only through your church website. Your call.
